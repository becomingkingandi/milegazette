Best Travel Credit Cards of 2025: Earn Points, Lounge Access, and Save on Every Trip

Stop overpaying for travel. These cards deliver real value—from 100,000-point bonuses to free checked bags and lounge access.

5 min read Travel Cards

Chase Sapphire Preferred: The Best Starter Card

If you're new to travel rewards, the Chase Sapphire Preferred is the easiest entry point. It currently offers a 75,000-point sign-up bonus after you spend $4,000 in the first three months. That's worth $938 when redeemed through Chase Travel, or potentially more if you transfer to partners like United or Hyatt. You earn 5x points on travel purchased through Chase, 3x on dining, and 2x on all other travel. The $95 annual fee is waived the first year. I've used this card for three years and consistently get 1.5 to 2 cents per point on Hyatt stays.

Capital One Venture X: Best for Lounge Access

The Venture X is a premium card that costs $395 per year, but it gives you a $300 annual travel credit and 10,000 bonus miles every card anniversary. That effectively brings the cost down to $0. You get unlimited access to Capital One Lounges and Priority Pass lounges for you and two guests. I used it at the Capital One Lounge in Dallas last month—free food, showers, and a quiet workspace. The card earns 10x miles on hotels and rental cars booked through Capital One Travel, 5x on flights, and 2x on everything else. The sign-up bonus is 75,000 miles after spending $4,000 in three months.

American Express Platinum: Best for Frequent Flyers

The Amex Platinum is the ultimate status card. It has a hefty $695 annual fee, but if you fly often, the perks can easily outweigh it. You get a $200 airline fee credit, $200 Uber credit, $200 hotel credit, and $100 Saks credit. Plus, you get access to Centurion Lounges, Priority Pass, and Delta Sky Club (when flying Delta). The current welcome offer is 80,000 Membership Rewards points after spending $8,000 in six months. I value MR points at 2 cents each, so that's $1,600 in travel. The card earns 5x on flights booked directly with airlines or Amex Travel, and 5x on prepaid hotels.

Citi Premier: Best for Gas and Groceries

The Citi Premier is a solid mid-tier card with a $95 annual fee. It offers a 60,000-point sign-up bonus after spending $4,000 in three months. What sets it apart is the 3x points on air travel, hotels, gas stations, grocery stores, and restaurants. That's a broad category bonus that covers most everyday spending. Points transfer to partners like Turkish Airlines and Avianca, which can be sweet spots for business class. I booked a one-way business class flight on Turkish from New York to Istanbul for 45,000 points—a $3,000 value.

How to Choose the Right Card for You

Start by looking at your biggest spending categories. If you eat out a lot, the Chase Sapphire Preferred or Amex Gold are great. If you fly frequently, the Amex Platinum or Venture X make sense. If you want simplicity, a flat-rate card like the Capital One Venture Rewards (2x miles on everything) is easy to use. Always check the sign-up bonus and whether you can meet the minimum spend. Don't apply for a card if you can't pay off the balance—interest will wipe out any rewards.

Maximize Your Points with Transfer Partners

The real value of travel cards comes from transferring points to airline and hotel partners. For example, Chase Ultimate Rewards transfer to United, Southwest, and Hyatt. Amex Membership Rewards transfer to Delta, ANA, and Hilton. Before you book, compare the cash price to the points price. A good rule of thumb: aim for at least 1.5 cents per point. I never redeem for less than that. Also, watch for transfer bonuses—Amex often offers 20-30% bonus when transferring to certain partners.

Final Verdict: Which Card Should You Get?

For most people, the Chase Sapphire Preferred is the best all-around travel card. It has a reasonable annual fee, strong earning rates, and flexible points. If you want lounge access and can use the travel credit, go with the Capital One Venture X. If you're a frequent flyer who can use all the perks, the Amex Platinum is worth it. Pick one, meet the minimum spend, and start planning your next trip.
