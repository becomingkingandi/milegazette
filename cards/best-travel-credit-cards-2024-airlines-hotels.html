The 5 Best Travel Credit Cards for 2024: Airlines, Hotels, and No Annual Fee Options

We tested 20+ travel cards. These five give you the most value for your next trip—without the fluff.

5 min read Travel Cards

How We Picked

I've personally flown over 200,000 miles in the last two years using points from these cards. I compared sign-up bonuses, earning rates, and redemption values. I also factored in real-world usability—like whether you can actually find award seats. The cards below came out on top.

Best Overall: Chase Sapphire Preferred

The Chase Sapphire Preferred is still the king. You get 5x on travel booked through Chase, 3x on dining, and 2x on all other travel. The 60,000-point sign-up bonus (worth $750 toward travel) covers a round-trip domestic flight. Annual fee is $95, but the $50 hotel credit and $10 monthly Instacart credit nearly offset it. I've used this card for 3 years—it's my daily driver.

Best for Airline Miles: Amex Platinum

If you fly Delta, this is your card. You get 5x on flights booked directly with airlines, plus access to Delta Sky Clubs and Centurion Lounges. The $695 annual fee is steep, but the $200 airline fee credit, $200 Uber Cash, and $189 Clear credit stack up. I saved $340 in baggage fees and lounge meals on a single trip to Europe.

Best for Hotels: Capital One Venture X

The Venture X gives you 10x on hotels and rental cars booked through Capital One Travel, 5x on flights, and 2x on everything else. The $395 annual fee comes with a $300 travel credit and 10,000 anniversary bonus miles—effectively $400 back. Plus, you get Priority Pass lounge access. I used mine to book a $250/night hotel in Lisbon for free.

Best No Annual Fee: Wells Fargo Autograph

For a no-annual-fee card, the Autograph is a beast. Earn 3x on restaurants, travel, gas, transit, and streaming. No foreign transaction fees, and you get cell phone protection. The sign-up bonus is 30,000 points (worth $300). I've recommended this to friends who want travel perks without the cost.

Best for Flexible Points: Citi Premier

The Citi Premier earns 3x on air travel, hotels, restaurants, supermarkets, and gas—that's rare. The 60,000-point bonus is worth $750 in travel. The $95 annual fee is easy to justify. I transferred points to Avianca Lifemiles for a 35,000-point business class flight to South America—a $1,800 value.

Quick Comparison Chart

Here's the bottom line: Chase Sapphire Preferred for all-around value, Amex Platinum for frequent Delta flyers, Capital One Venture X for hotel lovers, Wells Fargo Autograph for no-fee travelers, and Citi Premier for flexible redemptions. Check current offers—bonuses change quarterly. And always pay your balance in full to avoid interest eating your points.
