The 5 Best Travel Credit Cards for 2024: Ranked by Value

We tested 20+ cards to find the ones that actually pay off—here are the top 5 for points, perks, and plain value.

5 min read Travel Cards

How We Ranked the Cards

We didn't just look at sign-up bonuses. We crunched the numbers on earning rates, redemption values, and annual fees over a typical year of travel. Cards that made the cut offer at least 2x points on everyday spending and a clear path to free flights or hotels. We also factored in perks like lounge access and travel credits that can offset fees.

Chase Sapphire Preferred: Best All-Around

The Chase Sapphire Preferred remains the gold standard for most travelers. You earn 5x on travel booked through Chase, 3x on dining, and 2x on all other travel. The $95 annual fee is easily covered by the $50 hotel credit and the ability to transfer points to airlines like United and Southwest. In our test, a typical user got $1,200 in value in the first year alone.

Capital One Venture X: Premium Without the Sting

If you want luxury perks without a huge fee, the Venture X delivers. The $395 annual fee is offset by a $300 travel credit and 10,000 bonus miles on your anniversary—that's $200 in value right there. You get Priority Pass lounge access, 2x miles on everything, and a 75,000-mile sign-up bonus. For frequent flyers, this is the best premium card on the market.

Amex Gold: Foodie Favorite

The Amex Gold is built for people who eat out and grocery shop. You earn 4x points at restaurants and U.S. supermarkets, plus 3x on flights. The $250 annual fee is offset by $120 in dining credits and $120 in Uber Cash. If you spend $500 a month on food, you'll rack up 24,000 points a year—enough for a round-trip domestic flight.

Citi Premier: Underrated Points Powerhouse

The Citi Premier often flies under the radar, but it shouldn't. You earn 3x on gas, groceries, dining, and flights—that's rare. The $95 fee is a bargain for the earning potential. Plus, points transfer to 15+ airline partners, including Avianca and Turkish Airlines, which can unlock sweet spots like 30,000-mile business class redemptions to Europe.

Bilt Mastercard: For Renters Who Travel

If you pay rent, the Bilt Mastercard is a no-brainer. You earn 1x points on rent (up to 100,000 points per year) with no annual fee, and 2x on travel, 3x on dining. Points transfer to American Airlines, United, and Hyatt. In a year, a renter paying $1,500 a month earns 18,000 points—enough for a free night at a Hyatt or a short domestic flight.

The Bottom Line

Your best card depends on how you spend. For most people, the Chase Sapphire Preferred is the safest bet. If you want premium perks, go for the Venture X. And if you're a renter, grab the Bilt card. No single card is perfect—but these five cover every travel style. Apply before the sign-up bonuses change, and always pay your balance in full to avoid interest that eats your points.
