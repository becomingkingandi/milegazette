3 Credit Cards That Save You Money in Bangkok (No Foreign Fees)

Stop paying 3% per transaction in Bangkok. These three cards eliminate foreign fees and earn you miles on every pad thai and tuk-tuk ride.

4 min read Travel Cards

Why Bangkok Demands a No-Fee Card

Bangkok is a cash-heavy city, but cards work at 7-Eleven, malls, and hotels. Every swipe at a foreign ATM or register triggers a 3% fee on most U.S. cards. On a $1,000 trip, that's $30 wasted. These three cards kill that fee entirely.

Chase Sapphire Preferred: Best for Street Food and Hotels

Annual fee: $95 (waived first year). Earns 2x points on travel and dining. In Bangkok, that covers everything from $1.50 pad see ew at Thong Lor to $40 rooms at the Rembrandt Hotel Sukhumvit. No foreign transaction fees. Points transfer 1:1 to Hyatt or United—great for a free flight home.

Capital One Venture X: Best for Big Spenders

Annual fee: $395 (includes $300 travel credit and 10,000 anniversary miles). Earns 2x miles on every purchase. Use it for a $200 dinner at Sorn (booking required) or a $500 night at the Mandarin Oriental. No foreign fees. The $300 credit nearly offsets the fee if you fly once a year.

Wells Fargo Autograph: Best for No Annual Fee

Annual fee: $0. Earns 3x points on travel, dining, gas, and transit. In Bangkok, that means 3x on BTS Skytrain rides ($0.50 each), Grab taxis, and hotel stays. No foreign transaction fees. Perfect for budget travelers who want rewards without a yearly cost.

Real Numbers: What You Save in Bangkok

Average Bangkok trip: 7 days, $1,500 spend (hotel, food, transport, shopping). With a 3% foreign fee card, you lose $45. With any of these three, you save that $45 plus earn 3,000+ points (Venture X) or miles (Sapphire). That's a free meal at Jay Fai's crab omelet ($25) or a one-way flight to Chiang Mai (5,000 miles on United).

Pro Tip: Carry Cash for Small Vendors

Even with a no-fee card, keep 2,000-3,000 baht ($55-85) in cash for street stalls, taxis, and temples. Use your card at 7-Eleven for ATM withdrawals (fee waived) if you run low. Never use a standalone ATM—they charge 220 baht ($6) per withdrawal.
