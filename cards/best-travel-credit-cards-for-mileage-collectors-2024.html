5 Travel Cards That Earn You the Most Miles in 2024

Stop leaving miles on the table. These five cards earn you more on every purchase, with bonuses that can fund a free flight.

5 min read Travel Cards

Why Your Current Card Is Costing You Miles

If you're still using a standard cash-back or no-rewards card for everyday spending, you're missing out. A good travel card earns at least 2x miles per dollar on every purchase. Over a year, that's an extra 10,000 to 20,000 miles—enough for a domestic round-trip ticket.

Let's look at real numbers. The average American household spends about $60,000 annually. At 2x miles, that's 120,000 miles. At 1x, it's 60,000. The difference is a free flight to Europe or several domestic trips.

Chase Sapphire Preferred: The All-Rounder

The Chase Sapphire Preferred remains a top pick for beginners and seasoned travelers alike. It earns 5x on travel purchased through Chase, 3x on dining and streaming, and 2x on other travel purchases.

Current sign-up bonus: 60,000 points after spending $4,000 in the first three months—worth $750 when redeemed through Chase Travel. The annual fee is $95, but you get a $50 hotel credit each year, effectively lowering it to $45.

Points transfer to partners like United, Southwest, and Hyatt at a 1:1 ratio. That flexibility makes it a solid foundation for any miles strategy.

Capital One Venture X: Premium Perks Without the Premium Price

The Venture X has an annual fee of $395, but it pays you back. You get a $300 travel credit each year, plus 10,000 bonus miles on your anniversary—enough to cover the fee and then some.

Earning is straightforward: 2x miles on every purchase, no categories to track. Transfer partners include Air Canada, Emirates, and Singapore Airlines.

If you travel at least once a year, the card practically pays for itself. Plus, you get Priority Pass lounge access for you and two guests, which can save you $50+ per visit on airport food and drinks.

Amex Gold: Best for Foodies Who Fly

If your spending leans toward groceries and restaurants, the Amex Gold is a powerhouse. It earns 4x points at U.S. supermarkets and restaurants, plus 3x on flights booked directly with airlines.

Sign-up bonus: 60,000 Membership Rewards points after spending $6,000 in six months. The annual fee is $250, but you get up to $120 in dining credits (at partners like Grubhub and Cheesecake Factory) and $120 in Uber Cash annually.

Points transfer to Delta, British Airways, and other partners. For a couple who eats out regularly, the earning rate easily beats a flat 2x card.

Citi Premier: The Underrated Choice for Booking Travel

The Citi Premier earns 3x points on air travel, hotels, gas stations, and restaurants—all in one card. That's rare. Most cards force you to pick a category.

Sign-up bonus: 60,000 points after $4,000 in three months, worth $750 in travel through the Citi portal. The annual fee is $95, and there's no foreign transaction fee.

Points transfer to American Airlines, JetBlue, and Virgin Atlantic. If you book directly with airlines or hotels, this card's earning rate is hard to beat. Plus, you get a $100 hotel credit on stays of $500 or more booked through Citi's portal.

Bilt Mastercard: Earn Miles Without Spending on Rent

Rent is usually your biggest monthly expense, and most cards don't let you earn on it. The Bilt Mastercard does—up to 50,000 points per year on rent with no annual fee.

You also earn 3x on dining, 2x on travel, and 1x on everything else. Points transfer to over 20 partners, including United, American, and Hyatt.

Bilt's unique feature: you can pay rent with the card and avoid the typical processing fee. That's thousands of extra miles each year from money you were already spending. It's a no-brainer for renters who want to build a travel stash.

How to Pick Your Best Card

Start by looking at your biggest spending categories. If you eat out a lot, Amex Gold or Citi Premier. If you want simplicity, Venture X or Sapphire Preferred. If you rent, Bilt is essential.

Also consider your home airport and preferred airline. If you fly Delta often, an Amex card with transfer to Delta makes sense. If you're loyal to United, Chase is your partner.

Finally, don't apply for too many cards at once. Each application dings your credit score. Space them out by six months. And always pay your balance in full—interest will wipe out any miles you earn.
