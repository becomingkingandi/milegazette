The 5 Best Travel Credit Cards of 2024: Real Numbers, Real Perks

We broke down the top travel cards by annual fee, earning rates, and perks—so you can pick the one that pays for itself.

5 min read Travel Cards

Why Your Card Choice Matters More Than Your Destination

The right travel credit card can save you hundreds on flights and hotels each year. The wrong one can bury you in fees and points that expire before you use them. In 2024, the market is crowded. To help, we compared five top cards side by side, using real numbers and real redemption scenarios. Here’s what we found.

Chase Sapphire Preferred: The All-Rounder

Annual fee: $95. Earn 5x points on travel purchased through Chase, 3x on dining, 2x on other travel, and 1x on everything else. Points are worth 25% more when redeemed through Chase Travel, making them 1.25 cents each. A $500 flight costs 40,000 points. Add the $50 annual hotel credit and you’ve effectively cut the fee to $45. Best for: first-time travel card holders who want flexibility.

Capital One Venture X: The Premium Perk Machine

Annual fee: $395. Earn 10x on hotels and rental cars booked via Capital One Travel, 5x on flights, and 2x on everything else. The card gives you a $300 annual travel credit plus 10,000 bonus miles each anniversary—that’s $400 in value, so the fee pays for itself before you book a thing. You also get Priority Pass lounge access. Best for: frequent flyers who want lounge access without a $550+ fee.

Amex Platinum: The Luxury Heavyweight

Annual fee: $695 (rates vary). Earn 5x on flights booked directly with airlines and on prepaid hotels via Amex Travel. The card is packed with credits: $200 airline fee credit, $200 hotel credit, $240 digital entertainment credit, $155 Walmart+ credit, and more—up to $1,400 in annual value. But you must use the credits to make it worthwhile. Best for: travelers who use the credits and want elite status perks.

Citi Premier: The Simple Earn-and-Burn Card

Annual fee: $95. Earn 3x on air travel, hotels, restaurants, supermarkets, and gas stations. No complicated categories, no rotating bonuses. Points transfer to airline partners at a 1:1 ratio, and you get a $100 hotel credit on stays of $500 or more through Citi’s portal. A $500 round-trip domestic flight costs about 50,000 points. Best for: travelers who want straightforward earning with transfer options.

Bank of America Travel Rewards: The No-Fee Option

Annual fee: $0. Earn 1.5 points per dollar on all purchases. If you have a Bank of America checking account or are a Preferred Rewards member, you can boost that to up to 2.62 points per dollar. Points are worth 1 cent each when redeemed for travel statement credits. No blackout dates, no transfer partners. Best for: no-fee travelers who want simplicity and don’t care about lounge access.

How to Choose: Match the Card to Your Trip

Before you apply, think about your next three trips. If you fly domestic twice a year and value flexibility, the Chase Sapphire Preferred is your best bet. If you’re a road warrior who hits the airport monthly, the Venture X’s lounge access and travel credits win. If you want luxury and don’t mind juggling credits, the Amex Platinum is the top pick. And if you hate fees, the Bank of America card is a no-brainer. Always check the sign-up bonus—some cards offer 60,000 to 100,000 points, which can cover a round-trip flight or two.
