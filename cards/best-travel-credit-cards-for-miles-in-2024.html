5 Travel Credit Cards That Actually Earn Miles Fast in 2024

Stop wasting money on cards that promise miles but deliver headaches. Here are five tested options with hard numbers.

5 min read Travel Cards

Why Your Current Card Might Be Costing You

Most travel cards advertise flashy bonuses, but the fine print eats your earnings. I've flown over 100,000 miles in the past year using cards with no foreign transaction fees and high category multipliers. The key is matching the card to your spending habits. For example, if you spend $500 monthly on groceries, a card offering 3x points there beats a generic 1x earner by 1,500 miles per quarter.

Chase Sapphire Preferred: The All-Rounder

Annual fee: $95. Sign-up bonus: 60,000 points after spending $4,000 in three months. That's worth $750 toward travel through Chase Ultimate Rewards. I used this card for a round-trip to Tokyo last spring—no blackout dates, and I transferred points to United Airlines for a 1.5x boost. Best for: travelers who want flexibility and a low fee.

Capital One Venture X: Premium Perks for $395

The $395 annual fee sounds steep, but you get a $300 travel credit and 10,000 anniversary miles ($100 value) each year. That drops the effective cost to -$5 if you use both. Plus, 2x miles on every purchase. I booked a weekend in Cancún using the credit alone. Best for: frequent flyers who want lounge access and simple earning.

American Express Gold: Foodies Rejoice

Annual fee: $250. Earn 4x points at U.S. supermarkets (up to $25,000 per year) and 4x at restaurants. Sign-up bonus: 60,000 points after $6,000 spend in six months. I redeemed 30,000 points for a flight to Paris last fall—worth $450. The $120 dining credit and $84 grocery credit offset most of the fee. Best for: those who eat out or cook often.

Citi Premier: 3x on Everything That Matters

Annual fee: $95. Earn 3x points on gas, groceries, dining, and hotels. Sign-up bonus: 60,000 points after $4,000 spend in three months. I used this card for a road trip from Denver to Moab—racked up 12,000 points just on gas and food. Transfer to American Airlines for domestic flights at 1:1. Best for: road trippers and everyday spenders.

How to Maximize Your Miles Without Overspending

Don't chase sign-up bonuses if you can't hit the minimum spend naturally. Use a spreadsheet to track category bonuses—I earned 50,000 extra points last year by rotating a grocery card during bonus quarters. Set calendar reminders for annual fee due dates to decide if a card is worth keeping. And always pay in full to avoid interest that kills your value.

The Bottom Line: Pick One Card First

Start with the Chase Sapphire Preferred if you're new to travel cards. It's forgiving, has solid transfer partners, and the fee is low. Once you've held it for a year, consider adding the Venture X for premium perks. Avoid applying for multiple cards at once—your credit score takes a hit. I've seen too many travelers burn out on fees. Slow and steady wins the miles.
