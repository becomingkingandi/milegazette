7 Best Weekend Getaways from NYC (Under 3 Hours)

Skip the airport hassle. These 7 weekend getaways from NYC are all under 3 hours by train or car—with exact times, prices, and what to do.

5 min read Destinations

Why skip the airport?

Flying anywhere for a weekend means two hours at security, a delayed flight, and a rental car line. Instead, hop on Amtrak or drive. You'll be at a completely different scene in under 3 hours. Here are the best options, ranked by how fast you can get there and how much there is to do.

1. Hudson Valley (1.5 hours by train)

Take Metro-North from Grand Central to Beacon (about 1.5 hours, ~$30 round trip). Walk to Dia:Beacon, a modern art museum in a former factory. Then grab a farm-to-table lunch at The Roundhouse ($$). If you want hiking, Breakneck Ridge is a 20-minute drive—strenuous but worth it for the views. Book a room at the Dutchess Inn & Spa (from $150/night).

2. The Hamptons (2.5 hours by train or car)

The Long Island Rail Road from Penn Station to Southampton takes about 2.5 hours (from $45 round trip). Skip the summer crowds—go in May or September. Walk the Southampton Village, hit the beach (Cooper's Beach has a $20 day pass), and have dinner at The Lobster Inn ($$). For a budget stay, try the Southampton Inn (from $180/night).

3. Philadelphia (1.5 hours by train)

Amtrak from Penn Station to 30th Street Station is 1.5 hours (from $60 round trip if booked ahead). You're in the middle of the city. Walk the Rocky Steps, eat a cheesesteak at John's Roast Pork (cash only, $8), and see the Liberty Bell (free). Stay at the Alexander Inn (from $120/night) in Center City.

4. The Poconos (2 hours by car)

Drive from NYC via I-80 East to Delaware Water Gap (about 2 hours). This is your outdoor fix: hike the Appalachian Trail, kayak the Delaware River, or hit Camelback Mountain for skiing in winter. Rent a cabin through Airbnb (from $100/night). Stop at the Water Gap Diner for a classic breakfast.

5. New Haven, CT (2 hours by train)

Metro-North from Grand Central to New Haven (about 2 hours, ~$40 round trip). It's not just Yale—it's the pizza capital of the US. Go to Frank Pepe's (since 1925) for a white clam pie ($18). Tour the Yale campus (free) and catch a show at the Shubert Theatre. Stay at the New Haven Hotel (from $99/night).

6. The Catskills (2.5 hours by car)

Drive up Route 87 to Phoenicia (about 2.5 hours). This is for serious hikers: Slide Mountain (the highest peak) or the easier Kaaterskill Falls. After hiking, hit the Phoenicia Diner for a burger and a beer. For lodging, try the Graham & Co. in Phoenicia (from $150/night) or a campsite at Woodland Valley (from $25).

7. Newport, RI (3 hours by car)

Drive via I-95 to Newport (about 3 hours, no tolls). This one's a bit longer, but worth it for the Gilded Age mansions—the Breakers is a must-see ($25 entry). Walk the Cliff Walk (free) and eat fresh seafood at the Black Pearl ($$). Stay at the Newport Harbor Hotel (from $150/night).
