Lisbon on a Budget: How to See the Best for Under $100 a Day

Discover how to experience Lisbon's vibrant culture, stunning views, and delicious food without spending a fortune.

5 min read Destinations

Why Lisbon?

Lisbon has exploded in popularity over the past decade, and for good reason. The hills, the trams, the pastel de nata—it's a city that feels both historic and alive. But with that popularity comes rising prices. The good news? You can still have an amazing trip here without emptying your wallet. I've spent weeks in Lisbon and know exactly where to stretch your dollars.

Getting Around: The 24-Hour Transport Pass

Skip the taxis and Uber. Lisbon's public transit is efficient and cheap. A 24-hour Viva Viagem card costs just €6.40 ($7) and covers the metro, buses, trams, and even the famous Elevador de Santa Justa. I used it to ride Tram 28 through Alfama and the Bairro Alto—without paying the tourist premium. Pro tip: buy the card at any metro station kiosk and reload it as needed.

Free (or Nearly Free) Sights

You don't need to pay for a single museum to love Lisbon. Start at Miradouro da Senhora do Monte for a panoramic view of the city and castle—completely free. Then wander down to the Alfama district, where the narrow streets are the attraction. The National Pantheon costs €4, but it's worth it for the rooftop view. And the LX Factory, a trendy repurposed industrial complex, is free to explore and packed with street art and quirky shops.

Eating Well on a Budget

Food is where you can save big. Skip the tourist-trap restaurants near Praça do Comércio. Instead, head to the Time Out Market—yes, it's a bit touristy, but you can grab a gourmet pastel de nata for €1.50. For a full meal, look for 'prato do dia' (daily special) at local tascas. You'll find hearty dishes like bacalhau à brás for €8–10. My favorite spot: O Trevo in the Saldanha area—a local joint where lunch runs €7.50 and includes soup, main, and drink.

Where to Stay: Neighborhoods That Won't Break the Bank

Stay in Graça or Arroios instead of the historic center. Both are residential, safe, and well-connected to the metro. I found a clean Airbnb in Graça for €45 a night—a third of what you'd pay in Baixa. Hostels are even cheaper, with dorms starting at €15. If you want a hotel, check the Novotel Lisboa near the airport; it's about €70 a night and a 20-minute metro ride to downtown.

Day Trip: Sintra on the Cheap

Sintra is a must-see, but it can drain your budget. The train from Rossio Station costs €2.25 each way. Once there, skip the tourist buses and walk—it's steep but doable. The Moorish Castle is €8, but the views are just as good from the trail outside. If you want the Pena Palace, book tickets online for €14 to avoid queues. Pack a picnic; Sintra's restaurants are overpriced.

The Bottom Line

I spent a full day in Lisbon for €58 ($63)—including a tram ride, a full lunch, pastel de nata, a metro pass, and a castle ticket. With a budget of $100 a day, you'll have plenty left for a nice dinner or a fado show. Lisbon isn't the cheapest city in Europe anymore, but with a little planning, it's still a steal.
