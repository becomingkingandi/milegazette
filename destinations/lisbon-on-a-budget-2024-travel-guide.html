Lisbon on a Budget: 7 Ways to See the City for Under €50 a Day

You don't need deep pockets to fall for Lisbon—here's exactly how to do it on €50 a day.

5 min read Destinations

Why Lisbon Still Fits a Budget

Lisbon's reputation as a budget destination has taken a hit in recent years—hotel prices have risen, and touristy restaurants charge more. But the city's charm hasn't disappeared. You can still eat world-class pastel de nata for €1.20, ride historic trams for €1.65, and enjoy sweeping viewpoints for free. The key is knowing where to go and what to skip.

Sleep Smart, Not Pricey

Skip the Baixa and Alfama tourist zones. Instead, base yourself in Anjos or Arroios, both a 15-minute metro ride from downtown. A double room in a clean guesthouse here runs €45–60 per night. Hostel dorms go for €20–30. Check sites like Booking.com and filter for 'guesthouse'—you'll find family-run spots with character. Avoid weekend stays; prices jump 20% Friday to Sunday.

Eat Like a Local for Less

Lunch is your best meal deal. Many restaurants serve a 'prato do dia' (daily plate) for €8–10, including soup, main, and drink. Head to Rua das Portas de Santo Antão for classic options like Cervejaria Ramiro—but go at lunch, not dinner, to avoid the crowds and price hikes. For snacks, grab a bifana (pork sandwich) at O Trevo for €3.50, or a pastel de nata at Manteigaria for €1.20. Skip restaurants with English menus near Praça do Comércio; they're overpriced.

Get Around on Public Transit

Buy a Viva Viagem card (€0.50) and load it with a 24-hour pass for €6.40. This covers metro, buses, and trams—including the famous Tram 28. For views without the tourist price, take the 15E tram to Belém for €1.65. Walking is free and often faster in the hills. Use the 'Viva' app to top up, and avoid taxis unless you're splitting with friends.

Free and Cheap Sights That Wow

Start at Miradouro da Senhora do Monte for a free panoramic view. Then walk down to the Alfama district—wander the narrow alleys, no cost. Visit the LX Factory on Sundays; the market is free, and street art is everywhere. For €5, enter the Carmo Convent ruins—a haunting reminder of the 1755 earthquake. Skip the €20 Lisbon Card unless you plan to visit multiple museums in one day.

Day Trip: Sintra Without the Splurge

Sintra is a must, but you can do it cheaply. Take the train from Rossio station (€2.30 each way). Skip the €14 Pena Palace entrance—you can see the exterior from the park trail for free. Instead, hike up to the Moorish Castle (€8) for better views and fewer crowds. Pack a picnic lunch; tourist restaurants near the station charge double. Return by 5pm to avoid the rush.

The Bottom Line

With a €30 hostel bed, €15 on meals, and €6 on transit, you're at €51. Add a €5 museum and you've hit €56. But if you swap the hostel for a guesthouse and cook one meal, you can stay under €50. Lisbon's magic isn't in expensive tours—it's in the sun-drenched plazas and the sound of fado drifting from a doorway. Spend wisely, and you'll leave richer than you arrived.
