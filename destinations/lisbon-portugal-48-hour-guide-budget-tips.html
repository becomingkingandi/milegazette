Lisbon in 48 Hours: A Budget-Friendly Guide to the City of Light

Two days in Lisbon is enough to soak up the sun, ride Tram 28, and eat pastéis de nata without breaking the bank.

5 min read Destinations

Why Lisbon Now?

Lisbon is one of Europe's most affordable capitals. You can fly in from the US for under $500 round trip during shoulder season (April or October). The city is walkable, the metro is cheap at €1.50 per ride, and a pastel de nata costs just €1.20 at Manteigaria. I spent 48 hours here last spring and left with a full stomach and a nearly full wallet.

Day 1: Alfama and Belém

Start at Praça do Comércio, then walk up to Alfama. The São Jorge Castle costs €10, but the views are free from the Miradouro da Graça. For lunch, head to Belém—take Tram 15E for €1.50. Visit the Jerónimos Monastery (€10) and Belém Tower (€8). End at Pastéis de Belém for the original pastel de nata (€1.30 each). Total day cost: under €30.

Day 2: Baixa and Time Out Market

Ride Tram 28 from Martim Moniz to Campo Ourique. It's a tourist magnet, but worth €3 for a scenic loop. Disembark at Chiado for shopping and street art. Lunch at Time Out Market: a prego steak sandwich costs €9.50. Afternoon: explore LX Factory, a creative hub with free entry. Dinner at a tasca in Mouraria—try the bacalhau à brás for €12. Day total: about €35.

Where to Stay Without Overspending

Skip the pricier Chiado. Stay in Anjos or Arroios for €50–€80 per night on Booking.com. I booked a clean double room in a guesthouse near Intendente station for €55. The metro gets you to the center in 10 minutes. For hostels, look at the Independente Suites in Príncipe Real—dorm beds from €20.

Money-Saving Tips That Work

Buy a Viva Viagem card for €0.50 and load it with a 24-hour pass (€6.60) for unlimited metro, bus, and trams. Eat lunch at pastelarias—a sandwich and coffee for €5. Skip the expensive Fado shows in tourist zones; find free performances at Tasca do Chico on a weeknight. Water is cheap—buy a 1.5L bottle for €0.50 at a supermarket.

What to Skip and What to Prioritize

Skip the Lisbon Card unless you're visiting 5+ paid attractions—it's €20 and rarely pays off for a short trip. Prioritize the free viewpoints (miradouros) like Santa Catarina and São Pedro de Alcântara. Also skip the expensive river cruises; the ferry to Cacilhas costs €1.25 and gives you the same skyline views.

The Bottom Line

Lisbon in 48 hours is doable for under €100 per person, not including accommodation. The city is safe, the food is stellar, and the sun shines 290 days a year. Book your flight in advance, pack comfortable shoes, and bring an appetite for custard tarts. You'll leave planning your return trip before you even board the plane.
