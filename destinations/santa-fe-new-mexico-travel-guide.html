Santa Fe, New Mexico: The Complete Travel Guide for 2025

Discover the art, culture, and cuisine of Santa Fe with our insider tips and practical advice.

5 min read Destinations

Why Visit Santa Fe?

Santa Fe, New Mexico, is a unique blend of Native American, Hispanic, and Anglo cultures. At 7,000 feet elevation, it's the highest capital city in the U.S. The city is famous for its Pueblo-style architecture, world-class art galleries, and delicious Southwestern cuisine. With over 300 days of sunshine, it's a year-round destination. Whether you're an art lover, foodie, or outdoor enthusiast, Santa Fe offers something for everyone.

When to Go

The best times to visit are spring (April-May) and fall (September-October) when temperatures are mild (60s-70s°F) and crowds are thinner. Summer (June-August) is peak season with highs in the 80s but afternoon thunderstorms. Winter (November-March) brings snow and cold, but also lower hotel rates and festive holiday events like the Santa Fe Indian Market and farolito displays. If you want to avoid crowds, visit in January or February.

Getting There and Around

The nearest major airport is Albuquerque International Sunport (ABQ), about 65 miles south. From ABQ, you can rent a car (from $40/day) or take the Sandia Shuttle (around $35 one-way). Santa Fe also has a small airport (SAF) with limited flights. Once in Santa Fe, the historic downtown is walkable. For exploring beyond, like to the ski basin or pueblos, a car is helpful. The Santa Fe Trails bus system covers the city for $1 per ride.

Top Things to Do

Start at the Plaza, the heart of downtown, surrounded by shops and restaurants. Visit the Palace of the Governors (oldest public building in the U.S.) where Native American artisans sell jewelry. Explore the Georgia O'Keeffe Museum ($18 admission) and the New Mexico Museum of Art ($12). Don't miss the Loretto Chapel with its mysterious staircase ($5). For outdoor lovers, hike in the Sangre de Cristo Mountains or ski at Ski Santa Fe (lift tickets from $85).

Where to Eat

Santa Fe is a foodie paradise. For authentic New Mexican cuisine, try The Shed (entrees $15-$25) for red chile enchiladas. Café Pasqual's is great for breakfast (around $15). For upscale dining, Geronimo (entrees $35-$50) offers innovative dishes. Don't leave without trying a green chile cheeseburger at the Shake Foundation ($10). Also, sample local wines at Gruet Winery tasting room (tastings from $12).

Where to Stay

For a central location, stay near the Plaza. The Inn of the Governors offers Southwestern charm with rates from $150/night. For luxury, try the Rosewood Inn of the Anasazi (from $300/night). Budget travelers can find motels along Cerrillos Road from $70/night. Consider a vacation rental for longer stays; average $120/night for a one-bedroom. Book early for peak seasons.

Practical Tips

Drink plenty of water to adjust to the altitude. Wear sunscreen and a hat. Many museums are free on Fridays for New Mexico residents, but out-of-state visitors pay. Purchase a New Mexico CulturePass ($30) for admission to multiple museums. Tipping is standard (15-20%). Learn a few Spanish phrases; it's appreciated. Lastly, check for events like the Santa Fe Opera (summer) or Balloon Fiesta (October) to enhance your trip.
