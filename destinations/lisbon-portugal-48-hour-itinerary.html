Lisbon in 48 Hours: The Perfect First-Timer's Itinerary

Two days in Lisbon is tight, but with this plan you'll see the Alfama, eat the best pastel de nata, and still have time for a sunset drink.

5 min read Destinations

Why Lisbon?

Lisbon is Europe's sunniest capital, with more than 220 days of sunshine a year. It's also affordable. A nice dinner runs €15-€25, and a metro ticket is €1.50. The city's hills give you postcard views around every corner. For a first visit, 48 hours is enough to get a real feel—you just need a plan.

Day 1: Alfama and the Castle

Start in the Alfama, the oldest district. Get there by 9 AM to beat the crowds. Walk up to Castelo de São Jorge (€10) for panoramic views. Then wander down to the Lisbon Cathedral and the Miradouro de Santa Luzia for a classic photo spot. For lunch, skip the touristy spots near the cathedral and head to Rua das Flores for a bifana at 'O Trevo' (€3.50).

Day 1 Afternoon: Baixa and Chiado

After lunch, take the famous Tram 28 (€3) downhill to Praça do Comércio. Walk through Rua Augusta to the Elevador de Santa Justa (€5.50) for a quick view—or skip the elevator and use the free Miradouro de São Pedro de Alcântara instead. Spend the late afternoon shopping in Chiado. For dinner, book a table at 'Prado' (€35 for a tasting menu) or grab a seat at 'Time Out Market' (€15-€20) for a casual, high-quality meal.

Day 2: Belém and the Monasteries

On day two, head west to Belém. Take the train from Cais do Sodré (€1.50) or the 15E tram. Visit the Jerónimos Monastery (€10) and the Belém Tower (€6). The real highlight is the Pastéis de Belém bakery—the original recipe since 1837. Get there before 10 AM to avoid a long line. A box of six costs €7.50.

Day 2 Afternoon: LX Factory and Sunset

In the afternoon, go to LX Factory, a redeveloped industrial complex with street art, indie shops, and great coffee. It's a 10-minute walk from the Alcântara-Mar station. For sunset, take the 15-minute walk to the Miradouro da Ponte for a view of the 25 de Abril Bridge. If you're up for it, book a sunset sailing tour on the Tagus (from €30) for a different perspective.

Where to Stay and Getting Around

Stay in Chiado or Baixa for walkability. A mid-range double runs €120-€180 per night. Alternatively, the Alfama is charming but hilly—bring good shoes. The metro is efficient, but walking is the best way to discover the city. Buy a Viva Viagem card (€0.50) and load it with a 24-hour pass (€6.60) if you plan to use transit often.

Final Tips

Book key attractions online to skip ticket lines. Watch your belongings on trams and at miradouros—pickpockets operate there. Portuguese people eat late; dinner at 8 PM is early. Learn two words: 'obrigado' (thanks) and 'por favor' (please). They go a long way. With this itinerary, you'll leave Lisbon wanting more—and that's okay.
