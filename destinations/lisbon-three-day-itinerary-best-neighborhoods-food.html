Lisbon in 3 Days: The Best Neighborhoods, Food, and Views (Without the Crowds)

Skip the tourist traps and see the real Lisbon with this three-day plan that balances iconic sights with local favorites.

5 min read Destinations

Why Lisbon, Why Now

Lisbon has been on everyone's radar for a few years, and for good reason. It's one of Europe's most affordable capitals, with sunny weather most of the year and a food scene that punches well above its weight. But the crowds have grown, especially in the historic center. The trick is to plan your days around the less congested neighborhoods while still hitting the must-sees. This itinerary does exactly that, based on multiple visits and plenty of trial and error.

Day 1: Alfama and the Castle (Morning) + Mouraria (Afternoon)

Start early at São Jorge Castle. Go right when it opens at 9 a.m. to beat the tour groups. The views over the city are worth the €15 ticket. From there, wander downhill through Alfama's narrow streets. Stop at Miradouro de Santa Luzia for a postcard view, then grab a pastel de nata at Pastelaria Santo António (€1.20 each).

For lunch, head to Mouraria, the multicultural heart of the city. Try O Grade for grilled sardines (€12) or a bifana at O Trevo (€3.50). In the afternoon, explore the flea market at Feira da Ladra if it's Tuesday or Saturday. Otherwise, just get lost—Alfama is meant to be wandered.

Day 2: Belém (Morning) + Príncipe Real (Afternoon)

Take the 15E tram from Praça da Figueira to Belém (€1.50 with a Viva Viagem card). Visit the Jerónimos Monastery (€10) and the Belém Tower (€6). The real highlight is the Pastéis de Belém bakery, where the custard tarts have been made since 1837. Expect a line, but it moves fast. Get a box of six for €8.40.

In the afternoon, come back to the center and explore Príncipe Real. This neighborhood has boutique shops, art galleries, and the best park in the city: Jardim do Príncipe Real. For dinner, book a table at Taberna da Rua das Flores (mains around €15-18) or go casual with a prego at O Prego da Peixaria (€10).

Day 3: LX Factory (Morning) + Time Out Market (Lunch) + Sunset at Miradouro da Graça

Spend your last morning at LX Factory, a converted industrial complex with street art, indie shops, and cafés. It's a bit touristy now, but still worth a visit for the vibe. Grab a coffee at Copenhagen Coffee Lab (€3) and browse the bookshop Ler Devagar.

For lunch, hit the Time Out Market in Cais do Sodré. It's chaotic but fun—try the croquettes from Croqueteria (€6) or the seafood rice from Marisqueira Azul (€18). In the evening, make your way to Miradouro da Graça for sunset. Bring a bottle of wine from a local supermarket (€5) and enjoy the view over the city. It's free and unforgettable.

Getting Around and Saving Money

Forget the 28 tram—it's packed and slow. Instead, use the metro and buses. Buy a Viva Viagem card (€0.50) and load it with zapping credit. Each ride costs €1.50, and you can use it on trams, buses, and the metro. If you're staying for three days, €20 in credit is plenty.

Uber and Bolt are cheap (a 10-minute ride is about €5) and useful for getting to Belém or LX Factory. Avoid taxis from the airport—they charge a flat rate of €20, but the metro gets you to the center for €1.50.

Where to Stay Without Breaking the Bank

Skip the Baixa and Chiado, where hotels are pricey and noisy. Instead, look at Anjos or Arroios, just east of the center. These neighborhoods are up-and-coming, with great food and easy metro access. You can find a decent double room for €80-120 per night.

If you want to be closer to the action, Príncipe Real is a good compromise—quiet at night but a short walk to the main sights. For budget travelers, hostels in Alfama cost €25-35 per night and often include breakfast.

Final Tips and What to Skip

Skip the Fado tourist shows in the center—they're overpriced and inauthentic. Instead, try a local spot like Tasca do Chico in Bairro Alto, where you'll pay €10 for a drink and get real Fado. Also skip the Elevador de Santa Justa (€5 for a 30-second ride); the views are better from the free miradouros.

Do book tickets for the Jerónimos Monastery in advance to avoid the queue. And remember: Lisbon is hilly, so wear comfortable shoes. Your legs will thank you.
