48 Hours in Lisbon: The Perfect First-Timer's Itinerary

Two days is enough to fall hard for Lisbon—here's exactly how to spend them.

5 min read Destinations

Why Lisbon?

Lisbon is one of Europe's most affordable capitals—you can eat well for €10 and ride the historic Tram 28 for €3. It's sunny 290 days a year, and the city's seven hills offer postcard views at every turn. For a weekend trip, it's hard to beat.

Day 1: Alfama, Castelo, and Fado

Start in Alfama, the oldest district. Get lost in the narrow alleys before the crowds arrive. By 10 AM, walk up to Castelo de São Jorge (€10) for panoramic views. For lunch, skip tourist traps and head to O Velho Eurico for petiscos (Portuguese tapas)—try the fried codfish cakes. In the evening, book a fado show at Clube de Fado (€25 includes a glass of port). It's intimate and authentic.

Day 2: Belém, Time Out Market, and Miradouros

Take the 15E tram to Belém (€1.50). Visit the Jerónimos Monastery (€10) and the iconic Belém Tower (€6). Then, queue up at Pastéis de Belém for the original custard tarts (€1.20 each). In the afternoon, hit Time Out Market for lunch—try the bifana at O Prego da Peixaria. End your day at Miradouro da Senhora do Monte for sunset. It's free, and the view is unbeatable.

Where to Stay: Chiado vs. Bairro Alto

Chiado is central, safe, and close to everything—expect to pay €120–€180 per night for a double. Bairro Alto is livelier (read: louder) but cheaper, with rooms from €80. For a splurge, the Memmo Alfama offers rooftop views and a pool, from €250. Book at least a month in advance for weekends.

Getting Around: Trams, Elevators, and Your Feet

Lisbon is hilly—wear comfortable shoes. The Viva Viagem card (€0.50) works on trams, buses, and the metro. Top up as you go. The famous Elevador de Santa Justa costs €5.50, but you can skip it—the view from the top is the same as the free miradouros nearby. Uber is cheap (€5–€8 across the city) if your feet give out.

Money-Saving Tips

Eat lunch at local tascas—set menus (prato do dia) run €8–€12. Drink ginjinha (cherry liqueur) at A Ginjinha for €1.50. Visit museums on Sundays: many are free until 2 PM. And always carry cash—small shops and some restaurants don't take cards.

The Bottom Line

In 48 hours, you can see Lisbon's highlights, eat incredible food, and still have time to sit on a miradouro and watch the city glow. It's a destination that rewards slow travel—so don't rush. Just go.
