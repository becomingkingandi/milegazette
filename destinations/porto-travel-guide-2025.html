Porto Travel Guide 2025: Eat, Drink, and Explore for Under $75 a Day

Portugal's second city delivers big flavor, stunning views, and cheap prices—here's how to do it right.

5 min read Destinations

Why Porto Should Be Your Next City Break

Porto has everything you want in a European city break: medieval streets, a stunning riverfront, world-class wine, and food that won't break the bank. I spent five days there in March 2025 and averaged $72 per day—including a boutique hotel, three meals, and plenty of port tastings. Compare that to Lisbon, where the same trip would cost at least 30% more. Plus, Porto's airport is a 30-minute metro ride from downtown ($2.50), making it an easy weekend escape.

Where to Stay: Neighborhoods and Prices

For first-timers, base yourself in Ribeira or Baixa. Ribeira is postcard-pretty but noisy; Baixa is central and quieter. I stayed at Hotel Vera Cruz (Rua de Santa Catarina 100), a clean 3-star with breakfast for $65/night. If you want splurge, The Yeatman in Vila Nova de Gaia has river views and a wine spa, but rooms start at $250. Budget option: Porto Spot Hostel (Rua Gonçalo Cristóvão 12) has private rooms from $35. Book direct—I saved 10% by calling.

What to Eat and Drink (and What It Costs)

You can't leave without trying a francesinha—a gut-busting sandwich with sausage, steak, cheese, and beer sauce. Get it at Café Santiago (Rua de Passos Manuel 226) for $12. For cheaper eats, hit the Bolhão Market (Rua Formosa) for fresh produce and a bifana (pork sandwich) for $3.50. Dinner for two with wine at Taberna dos Mercadores (Rua dos Mercadores 36) runs about $50. And port tastings? Skip the big names on the Gaia side and go to Vasconcelos (Rua de Reboleira 10) for a flight of five ports for $15.

Getting Around: Walk, Metro, and Tram

Porto is hilly but walkable. I averaged 12,000 steps a day. For longer hops, the metro is efficient: a single ride is $1.50, and a 24-hour pass is $4.50. The historic tram 1 along the river costs $3.50 and is worth it for the views. Don't bother with taxis unless it's late—Uber works fine and a 10-minute ride is about $6. If you're heading to the beach at Foz, take bus 500 ($2) instead of the tram; it's faster and cheaper.

Top 3 Things to Do (That Aren't Just Wine)

1. Livraria Lello (Rua Carmelitas 144): The ornate bookstore charges $6 entry, but it's waived if you buy a book. Go at 9am to avoid crowds. 2. Clérigos Tower (Rua de São Filipe de Nery): Climb 240 steps for the best city view—$6. 3. Serralves Museum (Rua D. João de Castro 210): Contemporary art in a pink Art Deco villa, $12. On Sundays, it's free from 10am to 1pm.

Money-Saving Tips from My Trip

First, get a Viva Viagem card at any metro station ($0.50) and load it with credit—it saves 20% on rides. Second, lunch is the deal: many restaurants offer a prato do dia (daily special) with soup, main, and drink for $8–10. Third, skip the tourist menus on Ribeira; walk two blocks inland and prices drop 30%. Finally, port wine is cheaper at the source—buy a bottle at a local shop like Garrafeira do Carmo (Rua do Carmo 11) for $10 instead of $25 at a tasting room.

When to Go and What to Pack

May and September are ideal: warm, sunny, and fewer crowds than July–August. I went in March and had two rainy days out of five—pack a compact umbrella and waterproof shoes for the cobblestones. Also bring a refillable water bottle; Porto's tap water is safe and fountains are everywhere. For dinner, smart-casual is fine—no need for a jacket unless you're dining at a Michelin-starred spot like Antiqvvm (Rua de Entre-Quintas 220), where dinner starts at $120 per person.
