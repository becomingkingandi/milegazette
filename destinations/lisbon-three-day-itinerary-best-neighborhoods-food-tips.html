Lisbon in 3 Days: The Best Neighborhoods, Food, and Money-Saving Tips

Experience the best of Lisbon in 72 hours with this practical itinerary that covers iconic sights, hidden gems, and budget-friendly eats.

5 min read Destinations

Why Lisbon Now

Lisbon has become one of Europe's hottest city break destinations, and for good reason. It's affordable compared to Paris or London, packed with character, and the weather is mild even in winter. I've been three times in the last two years, and it keeps pulling me back. This itinerary hits the highlights without rushing, and it's designed for first-timers who want a real taste of the city.

Day 1: Alfama and Castelo de São Jorge

Start your trip in Alfama, the oldest district. Get lost in the narrow alleys, listen for fado music drifting from taverns, and climb to the Castelo de São Jorge for panoramic views. The castle costs €10, but the view is worth every cent. For lunch, head to Rua das Flores in the nearby Baixa district—try the bifana (pork sandwich) at A Casa do Bifana for just €3. In the evening, watch the sunset from Miradouro da Senhora do Monte; it's free and less crowded than other viewpoints.

Day 2: Belém and the Pastéis de Nata

Take the 15E tram to Belém, about 30 minutes from the center. Visit the Jerónimos Monastery (€10) and the Belém Tower (€6), both UNESCO sites. Then, do what everyone does: queue at Pastéis de Belém for the original custard tarts. They cost €1.15 each, and they're worth the hype. For a lighter lunch, grab a prego (steak sandwich) at Casa de Pasto in the nearby museum quarter. In the afternoon, rent a bike and ride along the riverfront back toward the city center.

Day 3: Bairro Alto and the Time Out Market

Spend your final day exploring Bairro Alto and the trendy Chiado area. Start with a coffee at A Brasileira, a historic café where Pessoa used to hang out. Then wander up to Príncipe Real for boutique shops and the garden at Praça das Flores. For lunch, head to the Time Out Market in Mercado da Ribeira. It's a bit touristy, but the food stalls are run by top local chefs. Try the fried cuttlefish at Marlene Vieira's stall—it's around €12. In the evening, catch a fado show in Bairro Alto; many venues offer dinner-and-show packages from €35.

Where to Stay Without Breaking the Bank

For your first visit, stay in Baixa or Chiado—it's central and walkable. I've stayed at the Lisbon Destination Hostel (dorms from €25, privates from €70) and the Hotel Lisboa Plaza (from €120 a night). If you want something quieter, try the Alfama neighborhood; it's more local and you'll find guesthouses like Casas do Bairro from €80. Book early—Lisbon fills up fast, especially in spring and fall.

Getting Around and Saving Money

Buy a Viva Viagem card (€0.50) and load it with rides; each metro or tram ride costs €1.50, and you can use it on buses and the ferry. The famous Tram 28 is fun but packed with tourists; take it early morning or use the 12E tram for a similar ride with fewer crowds. For airport transfers, the AeroBus costs €4.15, but the metro is just €1.50 and takes about 20 minutes to the center. Also, many museums offer free entry on Sundays—plan accordingly.

Final Tips for a Smooth Trip

Lisbon is hilly—wear comfortable shoes. Also, carry cash; many smaller shops and tascas don't accept cards. Learn a few Portuguese phrases; even a simple 'obrigado' goes a long way. And remember, meals are cheaper at lunch—many restaurants offer a 'prato do dia' for around €10. With this plan, you'll see the best of Lisbon without feeling rushed or overspending. Now go book that flight—you won't regret it.
