Portland, Maine on a Budget: $350 Weekend Guide with Real Costs

Discover how to enjoy Portland's lobster rolls, lighthouses, and craft beer scene for under $350, including real prices and insider tips.

5 min read Destinations

By the Mile Gazette Editorial Team

Reviewed against our editorial methodology · Updated July 28, 2026

Why Portland, Maine Is Worth Your Weekend

Portland packs a punch for budget travelers. You get coastal scenery, world-class seafood, and a walkable downtown. The key is planning ahead and knowing where to save.

Getting There and Getting Around

Fly into Portland International Jetport (PWM) from Boston or New York for as low as $80 round trip on budget carriers. Skip rental cars—Portland's Old Port is walkable. The metro bus costs $2 per ride, and rideshares average $8 within the city.

Budget Lodging That Doesn't Skimp

Stay at the Portland Hostel for $55 per night in a shared dorm. Private rooms start at $110. Book direct to avoid fees.

Free and Cheap Attractions

Visit Portland Head Light for free (parking is $5). Walk the Eastern Promenade trail for skyline views. The Portland Museum of Art offers free admission on Fridays from 4-8 PM.

Affordable Eats: Lobster Rolls and More

Skip touristy spots and hit High Roller Lobster Co. for a $12 lobster roll. Eventide Oyster Co. has $3 oysters. For breakfast, The Holy Donut sells $3 potato donuts.

Craft Beer on a Dime

Portland has 30+ breweries. Visit Bissell Brothers for $6 pints and free samples. Liquid Riot offers $5 tours. Stick to happy hour (4-6 PM) for $4 beers at most spots.

Final Budget Breakdown

Total: $400, but you can hit $350 with a hostel dorm and cheaper eats. Book flights 6 weeks out for best rates. Pack a reusable water bottle and snacks to cut costs further.
