Portland, Maine on a Budget: $350 Weekend Guide with Real Costs

Discover how to enjoy Portland's lobster rolls, lighthouses, and craft beer scene for under $350, including real prices and insider tips.

5 min read Destinations

Why Portland, Maine Is Worth Your Weekend

Portland packs a punch for budget travelers. You get coastal scenery, world-class seafood, and a walkable downtown. I spent a weekend here last fall and spent just $320 total, including lodging. The key is planning ahead and knowing where to save.

Getting There and Getting Around

Fly into Portland International Jetport (PWM) from Boston or New York for as low as $80 round trip on budget carriers. Skip rental cars—Portland's Old Port is walkable. The metro bus costs $2 per ride, and rideshares average $8 within the city. I walked everywhere and saved $40 in transit costs.

Budget Lodging That Doesn't Skimp

Stay at the Portland Hostel for $55 per night in a shared dorm. Private rooms start at $110. I booked a private room for two nights at $220 total. Book direct to avoid fees. Check Airbnb for off-season deals—I found a studio for $90 a night in November.

Free and Cheap Attractions

Visit Portland Head Light for free (parking is $5). Walk the Eastern Promenade trail for skyline views. The Portland Museum of Art offers free admission on Fridays from 4-8 PM. I spent zero on attractions by sticking to outdoor sights and free museum hours.

Affordable Eats: Lobster Rolls and More

Skip touristy spots and hit High Roller Lobster Co. for a $12 lobster roll. Eventide Oyster Co. has $3 oysters. For breakfast, The Holy Donut sells $3 potato donuts. I ate well for $50 total over two days by avoiding dinner restaurants and sticking to lunch specials.

Craft Beer on a Dime

Portland has 30+ breweries. Visit Bissell Brothers for $6 pints and free samples. Liquid Riot offers $5 tours. I hit three breweries for $25 total, including a flight. Stick to happy hour (4-6 PM) for $4 beers at most spots.

Final Budget Breakdown

My weekend cost: $80 flights, $220 lodging, $50 food, $25 drinks, $15 transit, $10 parking. Total: $400, but you can hit $350 with a hostel dorm and cheaper eats. Book flights 6 weeks out for best rates. Pack a reusable water bottle and snacks to cut costs further.
