Lisbon on a Budget: 48 Hours of Tram Rides, Pastel de Nata, and Sunset Views

Two days in Lisbon can cost less than €150 if you know where to eat, ride, and sleep—here's the exact plan.

5 min read Destinations

Why Lisbon Still Makes Sense for Budget Travelers

Lisbon has gotten pricier since the pre-2020 days, but it's still one of Western Europe's best-value capitals. A solid meal at a local tasca runs €10–€15. A beer is €2.50. The iconic Tram 28 costs €3 for a single ride or €6.50 for a 24-hour pass. Compare that to Paris or London, and you're saving €30–€50 a day. The key is skipping the tourist-trap restaurants in Baixa and heading to neighborhoods like Alfama and Graça.

Day 1: Tram 28, Alfama, and the Best Pastel de Nata

Start at Martim Moniz square before 9 AM to catch Tram 28 when it's less crowded. Ride it up through Graça and down to Alfama—it's a rolling postcard of azulejo-tiled facades. Hop off at Portas do Sol for a free viewpoint (miradouro). For breakfast, hit Pastéis de Belém's little sister, Manteigaria in Chiado—€1.20 per pastel de nata, still warm. Then wander Alfama's narrow alleys, ending at the Sé Cathedral (free entry). Lunch at O Tasco do Bernardo (Rua dos Remédios 118) for a €9 bifana and fries.

Day 1 Evening: Sunset at Miradouro da Senhora do Monte

Skip the crowded São Jorge Castle (€15 entry) and hike up to Miradouro da Senhora do Monte instead. It's free, higher than the castle, and locals bring wine and guitars at sunset. Grab a €4 bottle of vinho verde from a corner shop, sit on the stone wall, and watch the city glow pink. For dinner, walk down to Rua das Escolas Gerais in Alfama—try Cantinho do Aziz for a €12 moqueca (Brazilian-Portuguese fusion). Book ahead; it's tiny.

Day 2: Belém, LX Factory, and a River Cruise

Take the 15E tram from Praça da Figueira to Belém (30 minutes, €1.50 with a Viva Viagem card). Visit the Jerónimos Monastery (€10) or just admire its exterior for free. The real must-do is Pastéis de Belém—the original recipe since 1837. Get a box of six for €7.90 and eat them in the garden across the street. Then Uber to LX Factory (€6), a converted industrial complex with street art, indie shops, and the famous Ler Devagar bookstore. End with a €10 one-hour ferry from Cais do Sodré to Cacilhas—the skyline view from the water is unbeatable.

Where to Stay: Sleep Cheap, Sleep Central

Hostels in Lisbon average €25–€40 a night. Look at Home Lisbon Hostel (€30, includes free dinner) or Yes! Lisbon Hostel (€28, rooftop bar). For a private room, try a guesthouse in Graça or Anjos—expect €60–€80 on Airbnb. Avoid Baixa and Chiado if you're on a budget; you'll pay a 30% premium for the same room. Book at least three weeks ahead for summer weekends.

Money-Saving Tips That Actually Work

Buy a Viva Viagem card (€0.50) and load it with a 24-hour transport pass (€6.50) if you'll take more than two rides. Eat lunch at local tascas—the 'prato do dia' (daily plate) is usually €7–€9 and includes soup, main, and coffee. Skip the touristy Fado shows in Alfama (€25+); instead, catch free fado at Tasca do Chico in Bairro Alto (€5 drink minimum). And always carry cash—many small places don't take cards under €10.

The 48-Hour Bottom Line

Add it up: two nights in a hostel (€60), transport (€13), meals (€45), and activities (€10 for Belém pastries and ferry) totals around €128. Even with a splurge dinner, you'll stay under €150. Lisbon isn't the dirt-cheap secret it once was, but it's still a steal for a European capital with beaches, hills, and the best custard tarts on earth. Go now before the next wave of tourists discovers it.
