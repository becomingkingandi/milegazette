Lisbon in 48 Hours: A Local's Guide to the Best Views, Food, and Fado

Two days is enough to fall hard for Lisbon—here's how to do it right, without tourist traps or empty pockets.

5 min read Destinations

Why Lisbon, Why Now

Lisbon has exploded in popularity. And for good reason: it's sunny, hilly, and packed with character. But the crowds are real. The trick is knowing where to go and when.

I've spent months here over the years. This itinerary hits the highlights and the hidden corners—without the chaos.

Day 1: Alfama, Miradouros, and Fado

Start in Alfama, the oldest district. Get there before 9 a.m. to beat the tour groups. Wander the narrow alleys. Stop at Miradouro de Santa Luzia for the classic postcard view—it's free and worth it.

For lunch, skip the touristy spots near the cathedral. Walk five minutes to 'O Velho Eurico' on Rua de São Pedro. The grilled sardines (€12) and house wine (€2 a glass) are local staples.

In the evening, catch fado—traditional Portuguese singing. Book ahead at 'A Baiuca' (Rua de São Miguel 20). It's tiny, intimate, and authentic. Cover charge is €25, which includes a drink. You'll leave with goosebumps.

Day 2: Belém, Time Out Market, and Sunset Views

Take the 15E tram from Praça da Figueira to Belém (€3, or free with a Lisboa Card). Visit the Jerónimos Monastery (€10) and the Belém Tower (€6). Both are UNESCO sites and worth the entry fee.

Then, do what the locals do: eat a pastel de nata at Pastéis de Belém. The original recipe since 1837. Queue moves fast. One is never enough—buy two (€2.50 total).

Back in the city, hit Time Out Market for lunch. It's busy, but the food is top-notch. I recommend the croquettes from 'Croqueteria' and a bifana from 'O Prego da Peixaria'. Budget €15 for a full meal.

End your trip at Miradouro da Senhora do Monte. The view over the city at sunset is unbeatable. Bring a beer from a nearby supermarket and watch the sky turn pink.

Where to Stay Without Overpaying

Stay in Graça or Anjos for authentic neighborhoods and lower prices. A double room in a guesthouse runs €70–€100 a night. For a splurge, the 'Memmo Alfama' hotel has killer views and a pool—rooms from €200.

Book at least two months ahead for summer. Lisbon sells out fast.

Getting Around: Trams, Trains, and Your Feet

Walking is the best way to see Lisbon—but it's hilly. Wear good shoes. For longer hops, the metro is clean and cheap (€1.65 per ride).

Avoid the famous Tram 28 unless you enjoy being packed like a sardine. Instead, use the 'Viva Viagem' card (€0.50) and load it with trips. It works on trams, metro, and trains. You'll save money and time.

Money-Saving Tips That Actually Work

Eat lunch at 'pratos do dia'—daily specials at local restaurants. You'll get soup, a main, and coffee for €8–€10. Dinner costs double.

Skip the 'fado show' dinners that charge €50+. Go to a neighborhood tasca like 'Tasca do Chico' instead. You'll pay €15 for a glass of port and a performance that's raw and real.

Use the Lisboa Card if you plan to hit multiple museums. It costs €20 for 24 hours and covers public transport. It pays off if you visit three paid sites.

The Bottom Line

Lisbon delivers big on a budget. Two days, €150 a day (excluding accommodation), and you'll eat well, see the sights, and leave with a full heart.

Pack layers—mornings are cool even in summer. And don't skip the miradouros. They're free, and they're the soul of this city.
