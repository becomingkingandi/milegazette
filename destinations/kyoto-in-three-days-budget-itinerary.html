Kyoto in 3 Days: A $350 Budget Itinerary That Actually Works

A practical planning framework; verify current prices, terms and availability before making a decision.

5 min read Destinations

By the Mile Gazette Editorial Team

Reviewed against our editorial methodology · Updated July 28, 2026

Why Kyoto on $350?

Kyoto gets a reputation for being expensive, but it doesn't have to be. The key is smart planning: skip the pricey kaiseki dinners, use a bus pass, and hit temples with free or cheap admission. This itinerary works for solo travelers or couples.

Day 1: Temples and Bamboo (Under $30)

Start at Fushimi Inari Shrine—free and open 24/7. Arrive by 7:30 a. m. to beat crowds. Walk the lower trails for one hour, then head to Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion) for $5. 50 admission. Lunch at a konbini (7-Eleven onigiri costs $2). Afternoon: Arashiyama Bamboo Grove (free). Skip the paid garden inside—the grove itself is enough. Dinner at a local ramen shop near Kyoto Station: $8 for a bowl.

Day 2: Geishas and Gardens ($40)

Morning at Kiyomizu-dera Temple: $4. 50 entry. Walk down Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka streets (free). Lunch at a tofu restaurant in Higashiyama—set meal for $10. Afternoon: explore Gion district. Spot geishas around 5 p. m. near Hanamikoji Street. Don't pay for a geisha show; just observe. Dinner at a hole-in-the-wall okonomiyaki spot: $7. Total day spend: $40.

Day 3: Culture and Souvenirs ($35)

Visit Ryoan-ji Temple for its rock garden: $5. 50. Then walk to Ninna-ji Temple (same area, $5). Lunch at Nishiki Market—sample street food like skewers ($3 each) and matcha sweets ($2). Afternoon: free time at Kyoto Imperial Palace grounds (no ticket needed). Buy souvenirs at the market—fans start at $5. Dinner at a conveyor-belt sushi place: $10 for 8 plates.

Transport and Passes That Save Money

Get the Kyoto City Bus One-Day Pass for $6. 50. It covers all major temple areas. Skip the subway—buses are more direct. From Kyoto Station, use bus 205 to Kinkaku-ji. For Arashiyama, take the JR line from Kyoto Station ($2. 30 each way). Total transport for 3 days: $22 if you use the pass for two days and pay for train rides separately.

Where to Stay for Under $50 a Night

Book a hostel in the Higashiyama area. Private rooms start at $80. Other options: The Millennials Kyoto ($50/night, capsule style) or Budget Inn Kyoto ($40/night). Book two months ahead for best rates. Avoid hotels near Gion—they're triple the price for the same quality.

Three Practical Money-Saving Tactics

1) Buy a reusable water bottle—Kyoto's tap water is safe and free. 2) Skip the tea ceremony experience ($30+); instead, watch one at Nishiki Market for free. 3) Use the Kyoto Free Walking Tour (tip-based) on day one to get oriented.
