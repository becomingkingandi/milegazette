Lisbon on a Budget: 48 Hours of Food, Views, and Trams for Under $100 a Day

You don't need deep pockets to fall hard for Lisbon—here's how to do 48 hours right for under $100 a day.

5 min read Destinations

Why Lisbon Now?

Lisbon has exploded in popularity, but it's still one of Western Europe's best-value capitals. A pastel de nata costs €1.20, a tram ride €3, and a seat at a miradouro (viewpoint) is free. With direct flights from the US from around $500 round-trip and hotel rooms averaging €120 a night, it's a steal compared to Paris or London.

Getting Around: The Tram and Metro Hack

Skip the touristy Tram 28 (it's crowded and pickpocket-prone). Instead, buy a Viva Viagem card (€0.50) and load it with a 24-hour public transit pass (€6.60). This covers all trams, buses, and the metro. For the classic yellow tram experience without the crush, ride Tram 15 to Belém—it's less packed and drops you at the Pastéis de Belém bakery.

Free Views That Beat Any Paid Lookout

The best viewpoints in Lisbon don't cost a cent. Start at Miradouro da Senhora do Monte for a panoramic sweep over the castle and river. Then walk down to Miradouro da Graça for a postcard shot of the old town. For sunset, join locals at Miradouro de Santa Catarina—bring a beer from a nearby minimercado (€1.50) and watch the bridge light up.

Eating Well Without the Tourist Markup

Skip restaurants on Rua Augusta—they're overpriced. Instead, head to the Time Out Market for a splurge (€15-20 for a full meal), or better, dive into the backstreets of Alfama. A lunch menu at a local tasca (like O Velho Eurico) runs €10-12 and includes soup, a main, and a drink. For the best pastel de nata, go to Pastéis de Belém (€1.20 each) or Manteigaria in Chiado—both are worth the queue.

Day Trip to Sintra: Worth It or Skip?

Sintra is magical but crowded. If you go, take the train from Rossio (€2.30 each way) and arrive before 9 AM to beat the tour buses. The Pena Palace ticket is €14—book online in advance. Skip the Moorish Castle if you're short on time; the views from Pena are better. Alternatively, stay in Lisbon and take the ferry to Cacilhas (€1.25) for a seafood dinner with a skyline view—locals' secret.

Where to Stay for Less

Stay in Alfama or Graça for charm, or in Intendente for budget digs—hostels start at €25 a night, and boutique hotels like The Independente Suites in Chiado run €80-100. Avoid the Baixa/Chiado area if you want quiet; it's loud until 2 AM. Book at least a month ahead for summer—prices spike by 30%.

My 48-Hour Itinerary (The Short Version)

Day 1: Hit the miradouros, ride Tram 15 to Belém, eat at Pastéis de Belém, then explore Alfama's alleys. Day 2: Morning at São Jorge Castle (€10—go early), lunch in a local tasca, afternoon in the LX Factory (free entry) for street art and boutiques, and sunset at Santa Catarina. Total cost for one person, excluding accommodation: about €45. That leaves room for a fado show (€15-20) if you're keen.
