Lisbon on a Budget: The Perfect 48-Hour Itinerary for Under €100 a Day

Two days in Lisbon is enough to taste the best of the city—if you know where to go and what to skip. Here's how to do it right for less than you'd think.

5 min read Destinations

Start in Alfama Before the Crowds

Begin your first morning in Alfama, the oldest district. The streets are steep and narrow, but that's part of the charm. Walk up to the Miradouro de Santa Luzia for a postcard view over the terracotta rooftops. Go before 9:30 AM—tour groups arrive later, and the light is softer. Entry is free, and you'll have the place mostly to yourself. For a quick bite, grab a pastel de nata at Pastelaria Santo António for €1.10. It's a local favorite, not a tourist trap.

Ride Tram 28—But Skip the Long Queue

Tram 28 is iconic, but the line at Martim Moniz can stretch 40 minutes. Instead, walk to the Graça stop (a 10-minute uphill walk) and hop on there. The full ride costs €3.00 if you pay cash, but if you grab a Viva Viagem card (€0.50) and load it with a day pass, it's only €6.50 for unlimited trams, buses, and the metro. That pass pays for itself if you take just two tram rides and one metro trip. Ride to the Baixa stop, then walk to the Praça do Comércio for the riverfront view.

Lunch Like a Local in the Time Out Market

At midday, head to the Time Out Market in Cais do Sodré. It gets busy, but the food is worth it. You'll find stalls run by some of Lisbon's top chefs. A lunch of grilled sardines with bread and a drink runs about €12–15. For something quicker, grab a bifana (pork sandwich) at O Prego da Baixa for €5.50. The market has indoor seating, so it works even if the weather turns. Pro tip: go before 12:30 PM to snag a table without hovering.

Afternoon in Belém: Monuments and the Famous Pastry

Spend your first afternoon in Belém, a 20-minute train ride from Cais do Sodré (covered by your day pass). See the Jerónimos Monastery (€10 entry, but the outside is free and just as impressive) and the Belém Tower (€6, but you can skip the inside and enjoy the riverside walk). The real must-do is Pastéis de Belém, the original custard tart bakery. A tart costs €1.30, and the queue moves fast—even at 3 PM, you'll wait less than 10 minutes. Eat them warm with a sprinkle of cinnamon.

Evening in Bairro Alto: Sunset and Fado on a Budget

As the sun drops, head to Bairro Alto. The viewpoint at Miradouro de São Pedro de Alcântara is free and offers a sweeping view over the city. For dinner, skip the touristy fado houses that charge €40+ for a show and meal. Instead, find a small tasca like O Botas on Rua dos Remolares, where a plate of bacalhau à brás (cod with potatoes and egg) runs about €11. You'll hear fado drifting from nearby bars—just stand on the street and listen for free. Most locals do the same.

Day Two: Sintra or a Local Beach?

For your second day, you have two solid options. Option one: take the train from Rossio to Sintra (€2.30 each way with your Viva Viagem card). The Pena Palace is stunning, but the €14 entrance and the crowds can be overwhelming. Instead, hike up to the Moorish Castle ruins (€8) for better views and fewer people. Option two: if you prefer the coast, take the Cascais train to Carcavelos Beach (€2.15). It's a wide, sandy stretch with surf lessons starting at €25 for a group session. Both options are doable on a day pass.

Where to Stay and How to Save on Transit

Stay in the Baixa or Chiado area for walkability—you'll save on Uber rides. A double room in a budget hotel or guesthouse averages €65–85 per night in the shoulder season (April and October). For hostels, check the Lisbon Destination Hostel in Baixa; dorms start at €25. Avoid taxis from the airport—the metro's red line gets you to the center in 20 minutes for €1.80 with a Viva Viagem card. Buy the card at the airport kiosk. Add up your daily spend: two meals (€20), transit (€6.50), one attraction (€10), and a pastry (€1.30)—that's under €40, leaving room for a nice dinner or a fado CD to remember the trip.
