Lisbon on $100 a Day: A Perfect 3-Day Weekend Itinerary

Discover how to experience Lisbon's vibrant culture, stunning views, and delicious pastéis de nata without breaking the bank.

5 min read Destinations

Why Lisbon Is a Budget Traveler's Dream

Lisbon offers incredible value compared to other European capitals. A typical meal at a local tasca costs €10-15, and a glass of vinho verde runs about €2. Public transit is efficient and cheap—a 24-hour Viva Viagem card costs €6.60 for unlimited buses, trams, and metro. Even with a few splurges, you can easily stay under $100 (roughly €92) per day.

Day 1: Alfama, Castelo, and Fado

Start your trip in Alfama, the oldest district. Ride Tram 28 (€3 single or free with your 24-hour pass) up to Castelo de São Jorge. Entry is €10, but the panoramic views over the Tagus River are worth it. For lunch, head to O Velho Eurico for grilled sardines (€12). In the evening, catch a fado show at Clube de Fado—cover is €15, plus a drink. Budget total: €40.

Day 2: Belém Monuments and Pastéis de Nata

Take Tram 15 to Belém. Visit the Jerónimos Monastery (€10) and Belém Tower (€8). The real highlight is Pastéis de Belém bakery (Rua de Belém 84) where the original pastéis de nata costs €1.30 each. Grab two. For lunch, try the seafood at Mercado da Ribeira (Time Out Market)—dishes average €8-12. Spend the afternoon strolling along the waterfront. Budget total: €35.

Day 3: Chiado, Bairro Alto, and Sunset Views

Explore Chiado's bookshops and the iconic Livraria Bertrand (oldest in the world). Lunch at A Cevicheria (€15 for a ceviche bowl). In Bairro Alto, climb to Miradouro de São Pedro de Alcântara for free sunset views. Dinner at Ponto Final in Cacilhas—take the ferry (€1.30 each way) for €15 mains with a stunning Lisbon skyline view. Budget total: €37.

Money-Saving Transit and Accommodation Tips

Skip taxis and Ubers—public transit covers all major sites. Stay in neighborhoods like Arroios or Anjos for lower hotel rates (€50-80 per night) versus central Baixa (€100+). Use the Lisboa Card (€20 for 24 hours) if you plan to visit multiple museums—it includes free transit and discounts. Book accommodations at least 30 days in advance for the best prices.

When to Go and What to Pack

Visit in April-May or September-October for mild weather (20-25°C) and fewer crowds. Pack comfortable walking shoes—Lisbon's seven hills demand it. Bring a reusable water bottle; tap water is safe and free. A light jacket is useful for windy evenings by the river.

Final Budget Breakdown

Total for 3 days: accommodation (€180 for 3 nights in a budget guesthouse) + food (€90) + attractions (€35) + transit (€13) = €318, or about $106 per day. That's under your $100 daily target if you skip one café latte. Lisbon proves you don't need deep pockets for a deep cultural experience.
