Lisbon on a Budget: The Perfect 48-Hour Itinerary

Discover how to see Lisbon's highlights, eat well, and sleep cheap—all in a weekend.

5 min read Destinations

Why Lisbon?

Lisbon is one of Western Europe's most affordable capitals. You can eat a hearty meal for under €10, ride historic trams for €1.50, and stay in a central hostel for around €25 a night. The city's seven hills reward you with stunning views, and the food scene punches far above its price tag. In 48 hours, you can hit the essentials without rushing—if you plan smart.

Day 1: Alfama, Miradouros, and Fado

Start your morning in Alfama, the oldest district. Get lost in its narrow alleys before the crowds arrive. By 10 a.m., hike up to Miradouro de Santa Luzia for a postcard view of terracotta rooftops. It's free, and the azulejo-tiled terrace is a photographer's dream.

For lunch, duck into a local tasca. Try O Tasco do Vigário (Rua dos Remédios 150) for a €9 seafood rice that feeds two. In the evening, catch a fado show at Clube de Fado—book ahead. The 45-minute set costs €15 and includes a glass of port. Dinner nearby at a simple churrasqueira will run you €8 for grilled chicken and fries.

Day 2: Belém, Time Out Market, and Sunset Views

Ride the 15E tram to Belém (€3 return with a Viva Viagem card). Hit the Jerónimos Monastery (€10, or free on Sundays before 2 p.m. for EU residents). Then, queue for Pastéis de Belém—the original custard tarts. A box of six is €6.90.

Back in the city, spend your afternoon at the Time Out Market. It's touristy, but the variety is unbeatable. Grab a bifana at O Prego da Peixaria for €6.50. For sunset, climb to Miradouro da Senhora do Monte. It's the highest viewpoint, and it's free. Bring a beer from a corner shop (€1.50) and watch the city glow.

Where to Stay Without Splurging

Skip the Baixa tourist zone. Instead, base yourself in Graça or Anjos. These neighborhoods are 20 minutes on foot from the center and offer better value. Check out Home Lisbon Hostel (dorms from €22, private rooms from €60) or the independent Lisbon Lounge Hostel. Both include breakfast. If you prefer a guesthouse, look for a room on Airbnb in Graça—you can find private rooms for €35–€45 a night.

Getting Around: The Viva Viagem Card

Buy a Viva Viagem card at any metro station (€0.50 for the card, then load it). Each ride on metro, bus, or tram costs €1.50. The card is reusable, and you can top up as needed. For 48 hours, you'll likely spend under €10 on transit. Avoid the tourist passes unless you plan to use the elevator—the Santa Justa Lift is a fun ride, but the view from the top is just as good from nearby free miradouros.

Money-Saving Tips That Actually Work

Eat where locals eat. Look for lunch specials (prato do dia) that hover at €7–€9. Drink ginjinha, a cherry liqueur, at A Ginjinha for €1.40 a shot—skip the €5 tourist cocktails. Use free walking tours (tip what you can) to get oriented on day one. And always carry cash: many small tascas don't take cards.

Final Word

Lisbon rewards the flexible traveler. With a bit of planning, you can enjoy a full weekend for under €150, excluding flights. The city's real magic is in its miradouros, its trams, and its unfussy food. So pack light, wear comfortable shoes, and don't be afraid to get lost. You'll leave already planning your return.
