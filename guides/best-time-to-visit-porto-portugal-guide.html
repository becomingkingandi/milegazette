When to Visit Porto: A Month-by-Month Guide to Weather, Crowds, and Prices

From sunny riverfront picnics to cozy port wine tastings, here's when to go—and when to skip—Porto.

5 min read Guides

Why Timing Matters in Porto

Porto is a year-round city, but the experience changes drastically with the seasons. Summer brings packed terraces and beach weather, while winter offers quiet streets and cheaper flights. Your ideal time depends on whether you want to bake on the Douro or have the Ribeira to yourself.

Peak Season: June to August

Expect 25–30°C (77–86°F) and wall-to-wall tourists. Hotel prices jump 30–50% compared to spring. Book at least 2 months ahead for July. The São João festival on June 23 is a highlight—locals hit each other with plastic hammers and set off fireworks. If you go in August, you'll queue 30+ minutes for the Lello Bookshop, so arrive right at opening (9am).

Shoulder Season Sweet Spot: April to May and September to October

This is my favorite time. Spring brings 15–22°C (59–72°F) and blooming gardens. September and October still hit 20–25°C (68–77°F) with fewer crowds. You'll find hotel rates 20–30% lower than summer. The Douro Valley is spectacular in late September during the grape harvest—book a river cruise for €50–80 per person.

Off-Season Bargains: November to February

Winter is damp and chilly—expect 8–15°C (46–59°F) and frequent rain. But it's also magical: Christmas markets, mulled wine, and nearly empty museums. Flights from the US or UK can be 40–60% cheaper. The famous Francesinha sandwich (€10–15) is perfect comfort food after a cold walk along the Ribeira.

Month-by-Month Quick Guide

January: Coldest, cheapest, quietest. February: Carnival parades, still quiet. March: Warming up, but rainy. April: Cherry blossoms, moderate crowds. May: Ideal weather, first beach days. June: São João on the 23rd—book early. July: Hottest, busiest, priciest. August: Peak tourist season, expect crowds. September: Post-summer calm, harvest season. October: Still pleasant, lower prices. November: Rain returns, but cozy and cheap. December: Festive lights, fewer tourists.

Pro Tips for Your Trip

Always pack layers—even in summer, evenings cool down by the river. For the best light, visit the Dom Luís I Bridge at sunset (around 8pm in summer, 5pm in winter). To avoid crowds at the Clérigos Tower, go first thing in the morning or during lunch (12–2pm). And don't miss a port wine tasting at Graham's or Taylor's—book ahead for €25–35 per person.
