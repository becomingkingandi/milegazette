Best Time to Visit Bali: Beat the Crowds, Save Money, and Catch Perfect Weather

Skip the peak-season chaos and find your ideal Bali window—whether you want surf, sun, or serenity.

5 min read Guides

The Short Answer: April, May, and September

If you want the sweet spot of dry weather, fewer tourists, and reasonable prices, aim for April, May, or September. These shoulder months sit between the rainy season and the Australian school-holiday surges. You'll get blue skies, calm seas, and hotel rates up to 30% lower than July or August. I've been to Bali in May and September—both times I had beaches nearly to myself and never queued for a warung table.

Dry Season (April–October): Expect Sunshine, but Watch the Crowds

Dry season means reliable sun, but it's not all equal. June, July, and August bring the biggest crowds—especially in Seminyak, Canggu, and Ubud. Australians flood in during their winter break, and Europeans escape the heat. If you can only travel in these months, book accommodations 3–4 months ahead. For a quieter dry-season trip, choose late April or late September, when the weather is still perfect but the streets are less packed.

Wet Season (November–March): Lower Prices, Lush Views, and Rainy Afternoons

Don't write off the wet season. From December to February, rain often comes in short, heavy bursts—usually in the afternoon or evening. Mornings can be bright and perfect for exploring. You'll score deals: luxury villas in Ubud drop to $80–$120 per night, compared to $200+ in July. The island turns emerald green, waterfalls roar, and rice terraces look their best. Just pack a light rain jacket and plan indoor activities like cooking classes or spa days for the afternoons.

Month-by-Month Quick Guide

January–February: Peak wet, cheapest flights, lush scenery. March: Transition month—still rainy but fewer tourists. April: Dry season starts; best balance of weather and low crowds. May: Ideal—dry, sunny, and quiet. June–August: Busy and pricier; book early. September: Another sweet spot—dry, warm, and crowds thin out. October: Still dry but humidity rises; good for surfers. November–December: Rain returns, but Christmas week spikes in price and crowds.

Surf, Diving, and Festival Timing

Surfers: the best swells hit the west coast (Canggu, Uluwatu) from May to September. Divers: April to October offers the clearest water around Nusa Penida and Amed. If you want cultural events, plan around Nyepi (Balinese New Year) in March—the island goes silent for 24 hours, a unique experience. Galungan and Kuningan festivals happen every 210 days, so check the Balinese calendar for colorful temple ceremonies during your stay.

Budget Tips: When to Book and What to Pay

For the lowest prices, book from January to March (excluding Chinese New Year). Flights from the US or Europe can drop 20–40% compared to July. On the ground, a private driver costs $40–$50 per day in shoulder season, but $60+ in peak. A simple guesthouse in Canggu runs $25–$35 in wet season, $50–$70 in dry. Always book refundable rates if you travel in shoulder months—weather can shift, but flexibility pays off.

My Final Take: Go in May

If you only have one window, make it May. The dry season has just started, the island is still green from the rains, and the tourist wave hasn't hit. You'll find sunny days, warm 28–30°C (82–86°F) temps, and empty beaches. I've done Bali in both extremes—May is the month I'd recommend to any first-timer or repeat visitor. Book flights about 3 months out for the best mix of price and availability.
