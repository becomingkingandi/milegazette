Best Time to Visit Bali: Month-by-Month Guide (2025)

Skip the tourist traps and find your ideal Bali season—whether you want surf, sun, or serenity.

5 min read Guides

The Short Answer

For most travelers, the sweet spot is April, May, June, and September. You get dry weather, lower humidity, and thinner crowds. Flights and hotels dip about 20–30% compared to peak season. If you want the cheapest rates, come in February or March—just pack a rain jacket.

Dry Season (April–October): The Classic Choice

Dry season means blue skies and calm seas. Perfect for hiking Mount Batur at sunrise or diving in Amed. July and August are the busiest—expect Ubud traffic and Seminyak beach clubs packed. Book accommodations 3–4 months ahead. Prices spike 40% in August. If you want a balance, choose May or June: warm days, cool evenings, and still manageable crowds.

Wet Season (November–March): The Budget Play

Rain usually comes in short afternoon bursts, not all-day downpours. You'll save big—hotels drop 30–50%. Surfers love this time for the swell on the west coast (Canggu, Uluwatu). January is the wettest month, with about 15 rainy days. But you'll find empty temples and rice terraces. Just avoid the south coast beaches if you want calm water.

Month-by-Month Quick Guide

January: heavy rain, cheapest flights. February: still wet, but good for spa deals. March: transition month—rain easing, crowds starting. April: dry season begins, ideal for families. May: best overall—warm, dry, quiet. June: great weather, moderate prices. July: busy, but manageable if you book early. August: peak crowds and prices—book 6 months out. September: still dry, but crowds thin out. October: last dry month, good value. November: rain returns, but still doable. December: festive but wet and pricey.

Festivals and Events That Matter

Nyepi (Balinese New Year) falls in March 2025. The whole island shuts down for 24 hours—no lights, no noise, no airport. It's a unique experience, but you must stay indoors. Galungan and Kuningan (May and June 2025) bring colorful temple ceremonies. If you want to see them without the crowds, skip the south and head to Ubud or Sidemen.

Real Costs: What to Expect

In peak season (August), a mid-range hotel in Seminyak runs $80–120/night. In February, the same room drops to $50–70. A meal at a local warung costs $3–5 year-round. Surf lessons: $25–35. Scooter rental: $5–7/day. Budget $60–80/day per person in low season, $100–120 in high season, excluding flights.

Final Tip: Pick Your Region

Weather varies across the island. Ubud is cooler and rainier year-round. The Bukit Peninsula (Uluwatu) stays drier and windier. If you want consistent sunshine, base yourself on the east coast (Sanur, Nusa Dua) during wet season—they get less rain than the west. And always check the surf forecast if you're chasing waves.
