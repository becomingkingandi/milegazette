Budapest on a Budget: 7 Ways to See the City for Under $50 a Day in 2025

I spent a week in Budapest last spring and never spent more than $45 a day—here's exactly how you can do it too.

4 min read Guides

Why Budapest Is Still a Bargain

Budapest remains one of Europe's most affordable capitals. A beer costs $2 at a local pub, a full meal runs $8–$12, and public transit is $1.20 per ride. Even with inflation, prices haven't spiked like in Prague or Krakow. You can explore comfortably on $50 a day—including accommodation, food, and one paid attraction.

Where to Stay: Pest Side Hostels and Guesthouses

Skip the overpriced Buda Castle hotels. Head to the Pest side, District VII (the Jewish Quarter). I stayed at Maverick City Lodge for $18 a night in a dorm—clean, central, with free walking tours. Private rooms at Boogie Hostel start at $35. Book direct or use Hostelworld for last-minute deals.

Eat Like a Local: Market Halls and Street Food

Avoid tourist-trap restaurants on Váci Street. Hit the Great Market Hall (Nagy Vásárcsarnok) for lángos (fried dough with sour cream and cheese, $3) and goulash soup ($5). For dinner, try street food at Karavan Budapest near the ruin bars—a loaded burger costs $6. My favorite: the chimney cake at Molnár's Kürtőskalács for $2.

Free Sights That Beat Paid Attractions

Budapest's best views cost nothing. Walk up Gellért Hill for a panoramic shot of the Danube (skip the Fisherman's Bastion crowds). The Hungarian Parliament Building is free to admire from outside. On Sundays, the Buda Castle grounds are open without entry fees. I spent an entire afternoon exploring Castle Hill for $0.

One Paid Attraction Worth Your $12

If you splurge on one thing, make it the Széchenyi Thermal Bath. Entry is $12 on a weekday (cheaper than Gellért Baths at $16). Go at 7 a.m. to avoid crowds. The outdoor pools stay warm even in winter. Bring your own towel and lock to save $5 on rentals.

Getting Around: Public Transit vs. Walking

Budapest's metro, tram, and bus system covers everything. A 72-hour pass costs $12—less than $4 a day. I walked most days (the city is flat and compact), but used the M1 line for longer hauls. Avoid taxis; they overcharge tourists. Use the BKK app for real-time schedules.

The Bottom Line: Your $50 Daily Budget

Here's a sample day: dorm bed ($18), market breakfast ($3), metro pass ($4), free sights, a ruin bar beer ($2), and market dinner ($6). Total: $33. Add a thermal bath ($12) and you're at $45. With $5 left for a coffee. Book flights early—round-trip from the U.S. averages $600–$800. Budapest won't stay this cheap forever, so go now.
