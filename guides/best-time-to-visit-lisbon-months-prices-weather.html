The Best Time to Visit Lisbon: Month-by-Month Guide to Weather, Prices, and Crowds

Skip the summer chaos and save money. Here’s exactly when to go to Lisbon for the best balance of sun, prices, and peace.

5 min read Guides

Why Timing Matters in Lisbon

Lisbon is a year-round destination, but your experience changes dramatically depending on the month. July and August bring wall-to-wall tourists, hotel rates that jump 50% or more, and 30°C (86°F) heat. The flip side? January sees empty streets and bargain prices, but you’ll trade sunshine for rain. The sweet spot is late spring (May–June) and early fall (September–October). You get warm days, fewer crowds, and prices that haven’t peaked yet.

Peak Season (June–August): Sun, Crowds, and High Prices

June kicks off the high season. Festivals like Santo António (June 12-13) fill the streets with grilled sardines and music. By July and August, the city is packed. A mid-range hotel in Alfama that costs €120 in May can hit €200 or more. Expect queues at São Jorge Castle and the Tram 28. If you go, book 3–4 months ahead and avoid the midday sun—start sightseeing at 8 a.m. or after 5 p.m.

Shoulder Season (May & September–October): The Goldilocks Window

May is my pick. Average highs are 22°C (72°F), the jacaranda trees are in full purple bloom, and the city feels alive without being swamped. September is equally good—sea temperatures hit 20°C (68°F), perfect for a day trip to Cascais or Sintra. Hotel rates drop about 20% compared to August. You’ll still need to book a week or two ahead, but you’ll have room to breathe. October is the last month with reliable sunshine, though rain starts creeping in.

Off-Season (November–March): Budget-Friendly but Rainy

Winter is the cheapest time to visit. From November to February, you can find hotel rooms for under €80 a night in central neighborhoods like Baixa or Chiado. Flights from the US or UK often drop 30–40% compared to summer. But pack an umbrella—December and January see 100mm of rain per month. The upside: museums are empty, and you can score same-day reservations at top restaurants like Cervejaria Ramiro. Just bring layers and a rain jacket.

Month-by-Month Quick Guide

January: Cold (8–14°C), cheap, quiet. February: Still cold, but Carnival brings street parties. March: Warming up (15°C), some rain. April: Spring blossoms, Easter crowds. May: Perfect—22°C, jacarandas, low rain. June: Warm, festivals, prices rising. July–August: Hot (28–30°C), packed, expensive. September: Still warm (26°C), crowds thin out. October: Mild (22°C), rain returns. November: Cooler (17°C), low prices. December: Festive lights, but cold and damp.

Money-Saving Tips for Any Season

No matter when you go, these tricks cut costs. Stay in a neighborhood like Graça or Intendente instead of Alfama or Chiado—you’ll save 20–30% and still be a 15-minute walk from the center. Use the metro or trams instead of taxis; a single ride is €1.65. Eat lunch at a local tasca (small restaurant) where the 'prato do dia' (daily plate) costs €8–10. And skip the touristy Fado shows in the center—head to a local spot in Mouraria for €5 cover instead of €25.

The Bottom Line

If you want the best mix of weather, prices, and manageable crowds, go in May or September. You’ll get sunny days, lively streets, and hotel rates that won’t break the bank. If you’re on a tight budget, November and December are your friends—just pack for rain. Avoid July and August unless you love heat and crowds. Whichever month you choose, Lisbon’s hills, trams, and pastel de nata will make it worth the trip.
