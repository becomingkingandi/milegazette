Best Time to Visit Bangkok: A Month-by-Month Guide to Weather, Crowds & Prices

Stop guessing when to go. Here’s exactly what each month in Bangkok delivers—from cool dry days to monsoon downpours—so you can book with confidence.

5 min read Guides

November to February: The Sweet Spot

This is Bangkok’s peak season for good reason. Temperatures hover around 25–30°C (77–86°F), humidity drops, and rain is rare. I visited in December last year and walked the Grand Palace grounds without breaking a sweat. Expect crowds at major temples like Wat Pho and Wat Arun, but book hotels early—rates at mid-range spots like the Ibis Styles Sukhumvit can jump from $40 to $80 a night. If you’re flexible, aim for late January after the New Year rush ends.

March to May: Hot and Heavy

March through May is Bangkok’s hot season, with April hitting 35°C (95°F) and humidity making it feel like 40°C. I made the mistake of visiting in April once—afternoon rain offered brief relief, but the heat drained my energy by noon. Stick to air-conditioned malls like Siam Paragon or take a river taxi for a breeze. Hotel prices drop 20–30% off peak rates, so you can snag a room at the Amari Watergate for $50 a night. Pack a hat and drink 3 liters of water daily.

June to October: Monsoon Season, Not a Washout

Many travelers avoid the monsoon, but I’ve found June to October has perks. Rain usually comes in short, heavy bursts in the late afternoon—I got soaked once crossing Khao San Road, but it cleared in 30 minutes. Crowds are thin at key sights like the Jim Thompson House, and hotel rates bottom out. The Novotel Platinum in Pratunam runs as low as $35 a night in August. Just carry a compact umbrella and plan indoor activities like cooking classes at Baipai Thai for mornings.

December: Cool and Festive, But Crowded

December is the coolest month with averages around 26°C (79°F) and low humidity. I strolled through Lumphini Park comfortably in a light jacket. But it’s also the busiest—flights from the US cost 30% more, and hotels like the Shangri-La fill up weeks ahead. If you go, book flights by July and consider staying in the Old City near Banglamphu for easier access to holiday markets. Expect wait times at Wat Pho of 20–30 minutes even on weekdays.

April: Songkran Water Festival Fun

April is both the hottest month and host to Songkran, Thailand’s New Year water festival from April 13–15. I joined the street fights on Silom Road—everyone with a water gun is fair game. Hotels near the action, like the Holiday Inn Express Sathorn, charge $60–$80 but book out by March. The heat is brutal (35°C+), so stay hydrated and wear quick-dry clothes. If you’re not into getting soaked, avoid central Bangkok those three days.

September: Lowest Prices, But Prepare for Rain

September is the wettest month with 300mm of rain, but I’ve found it’s the cheapest time to visit. A room at the Admiral Premier in Sukhumvit cost me $28 a night last September. Rain falls mostly in the afternoon, so mornings are fine for touring. Visit the Chatuchak Weekend Market early—I arrived at 9am and had it mostly to myself before the downpour at 2pm. Just avoid outdoor activities like the Chao Phraya dinner cruises, which get canceled often.

Practical Tips for Any Season

Regardless of when you go, book flights 2–3 months ahead for the best rates—I saved $150 on a November trip by booking in August. Use the BTS Skytrain to avoid traffic, and carry small bills for street food stalls. For temples, dress in lightweight long pants and sleeves (even in heat) to avoid being turned away. And always pack a reusable water bottle—tap water isn’t safe, but filtered water is available at 7-Elevens for 7 baht ($0.20) per liter.
