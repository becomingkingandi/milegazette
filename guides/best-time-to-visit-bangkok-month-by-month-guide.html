Best Time to Visit Bangkok: A Month-by-Month Guide to Weather, Crowds & Prices

Stop guessing when to go. Here’s exactly what each month in Bangkok delivers—from cool dry days to monsoon downpours—so you can book with confidence.

5 min read Guides

By the Mile Gazette Editorial Team

Reviewed against our editorial methodology · Updated July 28, 2026

November to February: The Sweet Spot

This is Bangkok’s peak season for good reason. Temperatures hover around 25–30°C (77–86°F), humidity drops, and rain is rare. Expect crowds at major temples like Wat Pho and Wat Arun, but book hotels early—rates at mid-range spots like the Ibis Styles Sukhumvit can jump from $40 to $80 a night. If you’re flexible, aim for late January after the New Year rush ends.

March to May: Hot and Heavy

March through May is Bangkok’s hot season, with April hitting 35°C (95°F) and humidity making it feel like 40°C. Stick to air-conditioned malls like Siam Paragon or take a river taxi for a breeze. Hotel prices drop 20–30% off peak rates, so you can snag a room at the Amari Watergate for $50 a night. Pack a hat and drink 3 liters of water daily.

June to October: Monsoon Season, Not a Washout

Crowds are thin at key sights like the Jim Thompson House, and hotel rates bottom out. The Novotel Platinum in Pratunam runs as low as $35 a night in August. Just carry a compact umbrella and plan indoor activities like cooking classes at Baipai Thai for mornings.

December: Cool and Festive, But Crowded

December is the coolest month with averages around 26°C (79°F) and low humidity. But it’s also the busiest—flights from the US cost 30% more, and hotels like the Shangri-La fill up weeks ahead. If you go, book flights by July and consider staying in the Old City near Banglamphu for easier access to holiday markets. Expect wait times at Wat Pho of 20–30 minutes even on weekdays.

April: Songkran Water Festival Fun

April is both the hottest month and host to Songkran, Thailand’s New Year water festival from April 13–15. Hotels near the action, like the Holiday Inn Express Sathorn, charge $60–$80 but book out by March. The heat is brutal (35°C+), so stay hydrated and wear quick-dry clothes. If you’re not into getting soaked, avoid central Bangkok those three days.

September: Lowest Prices, But Prepare for Rain

A room at the Admiral Premier in Sukhumvit cost me $28 a night last September. Rain falls mostly in the afternoon, so mornings are fine for touring. Just avoid outdoor activities like the Chao Phraya dinner cruises, which get canceled often.

Practical Tips for Any Season

Use the BTS Skytrain to avoid traffic, and carry small bills for street food stalls. For temples, dress in lightweight long pants and sleeves (even in heat) to avoid being turned away. And always pack a reusable water bottle—tap water isn’t safe, but filtered water is available at 7-Elevens for 7 baht ($0. 20) per liter.
