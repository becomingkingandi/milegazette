Best Time to Visit Bali: Month-by-Month Guide for 2025

Skip the tourist traps and find your ideal Bali season, whether you want dry beach days, lush green rice terraces, or wallet-friendly deals.

5 min read Guides

The Short Answer

For most travelers, April, May, and June are Bali's sweet spot. You get dry weather, lower humidity, and fewer crowds than July and August. If you're on a budget, consider October or November—still warm, with occasional showers and hotel rates up to 40% cheaper than peak season.

Avoid December through February unless you don't mind daily downpours. That's the wet season, and while it's still hot (around 30°C/86°F), you'll face muddy roads and choppy seas for boat trips.

Dry Season (April–September): Best for Beach and Hiking

April to September brings Bali's dry season. Rain is rare, skies are blue, and the sea is calm—ideal for snorkeling in Amed, surfing in Uluwatu, or hiking Mount Batur at sunrise.

July and August are the busiest and most expensive months, with Australian school holidays and European summer. Expect crowds at Tanah Lot and Ubud's rice terraces. Book accommodations at least 3 months ahead if you travel then.

June is particularly pleasant: less crowded than July, with average highs of 28°C (82°F) and just 60mm of rain for the whole month.

Wet Season (October–March): Lush and Quiet

From October, rain returns, but it's not a washout. Showers usually come in short, heavy bursts in the afternoon, leaving mornings clear. The upside: waterfalls like Sekumpul and Gitgit are at their most powerful, and the rice terraces glow a brilliant green.

January is the wettest month, with around 340mm of rain—that's about 20 rainy days. But you'll score serious bargains. A beachfront villa in Seminyak that costs $250/night in August drops to $120 in February.

If you do visit in wet season, stay in Ubud or the east coast (Candidasa, Amed) where rain is lighter. Avoid the west coast beaches like Canggu, which get rough and dirty.

Festivals and Events to Plan Around

Bali's cultural calendar is packed, and timing your trip around a festival adds magic. Nyepi (Balinese New Year) in March 2025 is a unique 'Day of Silence'—the whole island shuts down, and even the airport closes. It's a one-of-a-kind experience, but plan for no activities, lights, or internet for 24 hours.

Galungan and Kuningan occur every 210 days, with the next ones in February and August 2025. You'll see beautiful penjor (decorated bamboo poles) lining the streets. Ubud's Writers & Readers Festival in late October is another draw, with authors from all over the world.

For surfers, the best waves hit from May to September, especially at Padang Padang and Balangan.

Budget and Crowd Calendar

Peak season (July–August, plus Christmas/New Year) sees hotel prices double or triple. A standard double room in a good guesthouse in Ubud runs $25–$35 in shoulder season but $60+ in August.

Shoulder months—April, June, and September—offer the best balance. You'll find reasonable rates and manageable crowds. For the absolute cheapest deals, come in February or March, but be ready for rain and some closed attractions.

Avoid the Easter school holidays (April 18–May 4, 2025) if you want to dodge Australian families. That period sees a mini-peak with higher prices.

Month-by-Month Quick Guide

• January – Wettest, cheapest, quiet. Great for spa retreats.

• February – Still wet, but fewer tourists. Good for yoga retreats.

• March – Rain tapers. Nyepi (March 29, 2025). Lush scenery.

• April – Dry season starts. Perfect for trekking. Moderate prices.

• May – My top pick. Sunny, dry, low humidity, and no big crowds.

• June – Dry and pleasant. Slightly busier. Book ahead for Ubud.

• July – High season. Great weather, packed beaches, high prices.

• August – Peak crowds. Expect traffic jams in Canggu and Seminyak.

• September – Still great, but crowds thin after the 1st. Good deals.

• October – Shoulder. Some rain, but still warm. Good for Ubud.

• November – Wet season starts. Low prices, but waterfalls are epic.

• December – Busy holiday period. Rainy but festive vibes.

Final Tips for Your Trip

Whichever month you choose, pack light layers—it's always warm, but evenings in the highlands can drop to 20°C (68°F). Carry a rain jacket if you're coming in wet season.

For the best experience, book flights mid-week (Tuesday–Thursday) and accommodations with free cancellation. If you're flexible, use Skyscanner's 'whole month' search to find the cheapest dates.

And remember: Bali's weather is local. If it's raining in Ubud, it might be sunny in Nusa Dua. Always check forecasts for your specific area before changing plans. Now go—Bali's waiting.
