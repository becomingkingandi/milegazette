When to Visit Lisbon: Month-by-Month Guide for Weather, Crowds, and Prices

From sunny beach days to cozy winter wine tastings, here’s exactly when to go—and when to skip—Lisbon.

5 min read Guides

The Short Answer: April–June or September–October

If you want warm weather without the crowds, aim for late spring or early fall. In May, average highs hit 72°F (22°C), and hotel rates are about 20% cheaper than in July. September is similar, with sea temperatures still around 68°F (20°C) for swimming. I’ve been twice in these shoulder months, and you’ll find shorter lines at São Jorge Castle and easier dinner reservations in Alfama.

Summer (July–August): Hot, Crowded, and Pricey

Lisbon in August is a furnace—expect 85°F (29°C) highs and packed trams. Hotel prices spike to an average of €180–€220 per night for a mid-range room, compared to €120–€150 in May. The upside: rooftop bars are buzzing, and the city’s famous Santos Populares street parties run through mid-June. If you must go in summer, book 3–4 months ahead and stay in quieter neighborhoods like Estrela or Lapa.

Winter (December–February): Mild Days, Cozy Nights

Winter is my secret pick for budget travelers. Average highs stay around 57°F (14°C), and rain comes in short bursts—not all-day soakers. You’ll see hotel deals under €100 per night, and the Christmas lights in Baixa are magical. Pack layers and a compact umbrella. Don’t skip a port wine tasting in Vila Nova de Gaia, just across the river—it’s an indoor activity that’s perfect for chilly afternoons.

Month-by-Month Quick Guide

January–February: Coldest and cheapest; great for museum hopping. March–April: Spring blooms, still quiet; bring a light jacket. May: Ideal weather and lower crowds—my top pick. June: Festivals ramp up, especially for Santo António on June 13; book early. July–August: Peak heat and prices; reserve everything in advance. September: Warm seas, fewer tourists; another sweet spot. October: Still pleasant, but pack a raincoat. November–December: Cool and festive; Christmas markets and fewer crowds.

Big Events to Plan Around

If you want to experience local culture, time your visit for a festival. June’s Santo António parades fill the streets with sardines and music—fun but loud. In October, the Lisbon International Film Festival draws cinephiles. For foodies, the Lisbon Fish and Flavours event in April showcases top chefs at affordable prices. Check the official Visit Lisboa calendar before booking—some events can double hotel rates overnight.

My Money-Saving Advice

Fly midweek (Tuesday or Wednesday) and avoid the weekend rush from European tourists. Use the Viva Viagem card for transit—it costs €0.50 for the card, then €1.65 per ride on the metro or tram. For accommodation, look in Anjos or Intendente, where boutique guesthouses run 30% less than Alfama. And always book refundable rates if you’re coming in shoulder season—weather can shift your plans.

Final Verdict: When to Go

My rule of thumb: If you want beach weather, go in September—the sea is warmest then. If you prefer exploring on foot without sweating, May is unbeatable. If you’re on a tight budget, December offers the lowest prices, just pack for rain. No matter when you go, Lisbon’s hills reward you with stunning views—just bring comfortable shoes and a sense of adventure.
